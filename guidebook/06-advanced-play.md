# BOOK SIX: ADVANCED PLAY

## CHAPTER 26: MULTI-SEEKER PLAY

### When Multiple Walk the Path Together

Temple of Gu was designed to work for **solo play** (one Seeker, one Guide) or **group play** (2-6 Seekers, one Guide).

Group play adds:

- Collaborative problem-solving

- Diverse perspectives and approaches

- Social dynamics and relationships

- Shared victories and struggles

- Community formation

**This chapter teaches you how to run Temple of Gu with multiple Seekers.**

### Character Creation for Groups

### Diversity Is Strength

When creating Seekers as a group, **encourage diversity**:

**Diverse Archetypes:**

- Don't have everyone be Healers or Warrior-Poets

- Mix knowledge-seekers, makers, protectors, and healers

- Different Archetypes see problems differently

**Diverse Lineages:**

- Represent multiple spiritual traditions

- Creates rich cultural exchange

- Prevents single-tradition dominance

**Diverse Virtue Spreads:**

- Not everyone should have +2 in the same Virtue

- Group should cover all four Virtues well

- Creates opportunities for different Seekers to shine

**Diverse Imbalance Patterns:**

- Some Seekers lean toward Doubt, some Fear, some Ego

- Creates interesting dynamics

- They can help each other with different challenges

### Session Zero for Groups

**Before the first session, gather everyone to:**

1.  **Establish relationships** - How do your Seekers know each other?

    - Fellow students in same cohort?

    - Brought together by an Elder?

    - Found each other organically?

    - Complete strangers assigned to work together?

2.  **Set boundaries** - What topics are off-limits for everyone?

    - Use safety tools (see Chapter 30)

    - Establish "pause" signals

    - Agree on comfort levels with conflict

3.  **Align expectations** - What kind of game do we want?

    - Collaborative vs. competitive?

    - Serious vs. lighthearted tone?

    - How much inter-party conflict is okay?

4.  **Create connections** - Give each pair of Seekers a shared moment

    - "Yara and Diego, you once saved each other during training. What happened?"

    - Build investment in each other's success

### Group Mechanics

### Lessons for the Group

When the group faces a Lesson together, you have options:

### Option 1: Everyone Rolls

- Each Seeker rolls their own dice using the relevant Virtue

- Everyone interprets their own outcome

- This creates diverse results (some succeed, some fail)

- **Use when:** Individual approaches matter, stakes are personal

**Example:** The group is researching in the Archive. Everyone rolls Oye. Maya gets 11 (finds crucial info), Kael gets 7 (finds it but gains Doubt), Diego gets 5 (finds nothing, gains Doubt). Different outcomes create complexity.

### Option 2: One Person Rolls, Everyone Shares Outcome

- Choose who's "taking point" on this Lesson

- They roll, everyone shares the result

- **Use when:** The group is working as a unit, outcome affects everyone equally

**Example:** The group is performing a Ritual together. Diego (the Healer) leads. He rolls Iwa. Gets a 9. The Ritual works but costs everyone 1 Imbalance (Doubt).

### Option 3: Collaborative Roll (Advantage)

- Two+ Seekers work together on the same Lesson

- Primary Seeker rolls 3d6, keep highest 2

- **Use when:** Teamwork makes sense, you want to reward collaboration

**Example:** Yara is trying to banish a hostile spirit. Kael helps by maintaining the protective circle. Yara rolls 3d6+Foriti, keeps best 2. Succeeds more easily because of help.

**Guide decides which option fits the situation.**

### Group Imbalance and The Crossroads

Each Seeker tracks their own Balance Track independently.

**But:** When one Seeker reaches a Crossroads, it affects the whole group.

### Option 1: Solo Crossroads (Default)

- The Seeker faces their Crossroads alone

- Other Seekers can't intervene directly

- But they can offer support, encouragement, witness

- **Use most of the time**

### Option 2: Witnessed Crossroads

- The Seeker faces Crossroads with the group present

- Group can offer spiritual support (each can roll Suuru to help)

- Successful support rolls reduce the severity or provide advantage

- **Use when:** The group is tight-knit and wants to support each other

### Option 3: Group Crossroads (Rare)

- The entire group has filled their tracks simultaneously

- They face a collective Crossroads together

- This is a MAJOR moment—use sparingly

- **Use when:** A massive crisis affects everyone equally

### Shared Ebo

Groups can perform Ebo together, which is more powerful than solo practice.

**Communal Ebo (Everyone Benefits):**

- The group gathers to perform Ebo as a community

- Each person offers something and rolls

- **On 10+:** Clear 1 Imbalance, plus everyone gets +1 to resist Imbalance for the next scene

- **On 7-9:** Clear 1 Imbalance normally

- **On 6-:** Shared Ebo failed, the group must try differently together

**Use this for:**

- Regular group meditation or prayer

- Shared meals offered as Ebo

- Collaborative service projects

- Group Rituals

**Supporting Someone's Ebo:**

- One Seeker performs Ebo, others support

- Supporting Seekers roll Suuru

- **On 10+:** Primary Seeker gets +1 to their Ebo roll

- **Multiple supporters:** Bonuses stack (max +3)

**Example:** Diego is performing deep healing Ebo after witnessing trauma. Maya and Yara sit with him in support. Both roll Suuru. Maya gets 11, Yara gets 8. Diego gets +2 to his Ebo roll.

### Group Mysteries

When a group works on a Mystery together:

**Shared Clock:**

- One Clock tracks the Mystery progress

- Any Seeker's successful Lesson can advance it

- Everyone earns the Breakthrough when complete

**Breakthrough Rewards:**

- Each Seeker chooses their own reward

- Rewards can be the same or different

- Guide can offer group rewards (everyone learns the same Ritual, all get reputation with a faction, etc.)

**Splitting the Party:**

- Sometimes Seekers split up to investigate different leads

- Track progress separately, then converge

- Both paths can fill the Clock

- Don't punish splitting—sometimes it's smart

### Group Dynamics and Conflict

### Healthy Conflict

Some inter-character conflict is good:

- Different approaches to problems

- Philosophical disagreements

- Competing priorities

- Personality clAses

**This creates drama and depth.**

**But set limits:**

- No PvP combat without everyone's consent

- No stealing from each other

- No sabotaging each other's Mysteries

- Resolve conflicts in-character when possible

### Unhealthy Conflict

Watch for:

- One player dominating all decisions

- Players ganging up on one Seeker

- Out-of-character grudges bleeding into the game

- Passive-aggressive play

**If you see this, pause the game and address it directly.**

### Spotlight Management

### Everyone Gets Time

With multiple Seekers, **manage spotlight time deliberately.**

**Techniques:**

### 1. Rotating Focus

- Each session, one Seeker is "primary focus"

- Their personal Mystery or struggle takes center stage

- Others support or have subplots

- Next session, rotate to someone else

### 2. Scene Framing

- "Maya, let's start with you. What are you doing?"

- After her scene concludes: "Kael, we cut to you. Where are you?"

- Ensure everyone gets at least 2-3 scenes per session

### 3. Ensemble Moments

- Create scenes where everyone has something to do

- "You're all at the Community Hearth. Go around—what's each of you working on?"

### 4. Paired Scenes

- Two Seekers have a private conversation

- Gives intimacy, prevents overcrowding

- "Maya and Diego, you're walking together. What do you talk about?"

### When Someone's Quiet

If a player is being quiet:

- **Check in:** "Kael, what's your Seeker thinking right now?"

- **Direct invitation:** "Kael, this seems like something a Seer would notice. What do you see?"

- **Respect boundaries:** Some people need quiet time. That's okay.

### Collaborative Rituals

### Rituals Multiple People Can Perform Together

Some Rituals are enhanced by group performance:

**Ritual of the Healing Circle** (designed for groups)

- 3-8 people required

- Everyone shares, everyone witnesses

- All clear 1 Imbalance on success

**Ritual of Community Healing** (designed for groups)

- Organizes large gatherings

- Benefits entire community

**Any Ritual can be collaborative:**

- Choose one person as primary performer

- Others assist (provide materials, energy, witness)

- Assistants can roll to support (see Shared Ebo rules)

### Group-Specific Challenges

### Challenge 1: Pacing

With more people, everything takes longer.

**Solutions:**

- Timebox scenes ("We'll spend 15 minutes on this investigation")

- Cut between scenes more frequently

- Fast-forward through routine tasks

- Not everything needs to be roleplayed in real-time

### Challenge 2: Decision Paralysis

Groups can get stuck debating what to do.

**Solutions:**

- Set a "discussion time limit" then someone decides

- Use a quick vote if stuck

- Guide can say: "You debate for a while. What do you decide?"

- Encourage "failing forward"—doing something imperfect is better than doing nothing

### Challenge 3: Split Attention

With 4+ Seekers, some will zone out during others' scenes.

**Solutions:**

- Keep scenes punchy and engaging

- Give spectating players jobs ("You're watching this. What does your Seeker think?")

- Allow side conversations during downtime scenes

- Take breaks

### Challenge 4: Power Imbalance

One Seeker is mechanically stronger or has hoarded resources.

**Solutions:**

- Mysteries should require diverse skills

- Give weaker Seekers spotlight opportunities

- Remind the strong Seeker: this is collaborative, not competitive

- Consider adjusting Breakthrough rewards to balance the group

### Party Composition Archetypes

### The Balanced Party (Recommended for new groups)

- 1 Healer (clears Imbalance)

- 1 Knowledge-Seeker (Chronicler or Seer - investigation)

- 1 Maker (Artisan or Code-Smith - problem-solving)

- 1 Protector (Warrior-Poet - handles danger)

**Strengths:** Can handle most situations  
**Weaknesses:** Can feel formulaic

### The Specialists

- All same Archetype or closely related

- Example: All Healers, or all Knowledge-Seekers

**Strengths:** Deep expertise, interesting niche play  
**Weaknesses:** Struggles with problems outside specialty

### The Chaos Party

- Random Archetypes with no thought to balance

- Whatever players want to play

**Strengths:** Maximum player agency and fun  
**Weaknesses:** May struggle with some challenges

**All compositions work.** Adapt Mysteries to the party's strengths and weaknesses.

### Remote Play Considerations

If playing online (Discord, Zoom, Roll20, etc.):

**Challenges:**

- Harder to read body language

- Technical issues disrupt flow

- Simultaneous conversation is chaotic

**Solutions:**

- Use video when possible

- Establish "raise hand" protocol for speaking

- Use digital tools for rolls and tracking (shared spreadsheets, Roll20, Owlbear Rodeo)

- Take more frequent breaks

- Check in explicitly: "Everyone good? Need anything?"

### Group Ebo Examples

### Example 1: Weekly Group Meditation

**Setup:** Every Saturday morning, the whole group does shared meditation.

**Mechanics:** Everyone rolls Suuru.

- **10+:** Clear 1 Imbalance, feel centered

- **7-9:** Clear 1 Imbalance but something surfaces (gain 1 Doubt about something unrelated)

- **6-:** No clearing, distraction prevents centering

**Narrative:** Describe the shared space, the energy, what each person's practice looks like.

### Example 2: Community Service

**Setup:** The group volunteers at a local shelter.

**Mechanics:** Each person rolls Iwa (service) or Foriti (sustained physical work).

- **10+:** Clear 1 Imbalance, feel good about helping

- **7-9:** Clear 1 Imbalance but exhausted (mark 1 Fear)

- **6-:** Service is hollow or resentful, no clearing

**Narrative:** Show the impact of their service, the people they help.

### Final Advice for Group Play

**Celebrate each other.** When someone has a Breakthrough, everyone celebrates.

**Support each other.** When someone's struggling, offer help.

**Create traditions.** Group rituals, inside jokes, shared history.

**Respect differences.** Different playstyles are okay.

**Communicate openly.** Problems don't fix themselves.

**Have fun together.** That's the whole point.

## CHAPTER 27: THE SACRED ECONOMY

### Money vs. Sacred Exchange

The Temple doesn't use money internally. Instead, it operates on systems of **reciprocity, service, and sacred exchange.**

### Why no money?

- Money commodifies relationships

- The practice isn't for sale

- Prevents wealth-based hierarchy

- Aligns with Ebo philosophy (reciprocity, not transaction)

**But resources matter:**

- Materials for crafting Sacred Implements

- Time and attention from Elders

- Access to restricted knowledge

- Ritual components

- Food, shelter, clothing

**This chapter explains how resources work in Temple of Gu.**

### TEMPLE CONTRIBUTION POINTS (TCP)

### What Are TCP?

**Temple Contribution Points** represent your **demonstrated commitment** to the community through service and practice.

**You earn TCP by:**

- Teaching classes or mentoring junior Seekers

- Maintaining Temple infrastructure (Archive, gardens, network)

- Performing community service

- Completing faction missions

- Participating in communal Rituals and events

- Creating things that benefit others

**You spend TCP on:**

- Advanced training from Elders

- Access to restricted resources or locations

- Borrowing Sacred Implements

- Materials for crafting

- Requesting specific assistance

**TCP is not currency.** It's a reputation/contribution tracking system.

### Earning TCP

**Standard Activities:**

- Weekly archive maintenance: 1 TCP

- Teaching a class: 2-5 TCP (depending on quality)

- Completing a faction mission: 1-3 TCP (varies by difficulty)

- Organizing a community event: 3-5 TCP

- Creating a useful tool/resource for the Temple: 2-10 TCP

**Guide determines exact awards based on:**

- Effort invested

- Impact on community

- Quality of work

- Whether it was requested or voluntary

**You can't grind TCP.** The Guide can cap earnings (max 5-10 TCP per session) to prevent hoarding.

### Spending TCP

**Common Costs:**

- Advanced training session with Elder: 3-5 TCP

- Borrowing a Sacred Implement for a mission: 1-3 TCP

- Access to restricted Archive section for research: 2 TCP

- Commissioning an Artisan to create something: 5-10 TCP

- Requesting an Elder's time for personal Mystery help: 3-5 TCP

**TCP doesn't guarantee cooperation.** Even if you have the points, NPCs can refuse (though they usually won't unless you've offended them).

### Starting TCP

New Seekers start with **5 TCP** (goodwill for joining the Temple).

### SPIRIT FAVORS

### What Are Spirit Favors?

**Spirit Favors** represent **positive relationships with individual spirits** that you can call upon when needed.

**You earn Spirit Favors by:**

- Performing Ebo specifically for a spirit

- Helping a spirit complete its unfinished business

- Regular offerings and attention to a specific spirit

- Treating spirits with exceptional respect

- Completing Mysteries that help spirits

**You spend Spirit Favors on:**

- Asking a spirit for help with a specific problem

- Requesting the spirit's presence during a Ritual

- Gaining access to knowledge the spirit holds

- Protection during a dangerous journey

- Information about other spirits or spiritual matters

### Earning Spirit Favors

**Specific actions:**

- Performing the Ritual of Imbue Spirit: Gain 1 Favor with that spirit

- Completing a Mystery that helps a spirit: Gain 1-3 Favors

- Regular offerings (weekly for a month): Gain 1 Favor

- Saving a spirit from harm/corruption: Gain 2-3 Favors

- Performing the Ritual of the Eternal Bond: Gain 5 Favors (maximum devotion)

**Track Spirit Favors individually.** You might have:

- 3 Favors with the Archive Keeper

- 1 Favor with a river-spirit

- 5 Favors with your personal spirit-guide

### Spending Spirit Favors

**Request help:**

- Spend 1 Favor: Spirit helps with minor task (information, small favor)

- Spend 2-3 Favors: Spirit helps with significant task (protection, major insight)

- Spend 5 Favors: Spirit commits to major ongoing support

**The spirit can refuse** if:

- What you're asking is against its nature

- What you're asking would harm it

- You've neglected the relationship recently

- You're asking too much too often

**After spending Favors, rebuild the relationship through more Ebo.**

### MATERIAL RESOURCES

### What's Valuable?

In the Temple's economy, certain materials are valuable for crafting Sacred Implements and performing Rituals:

**Natural Materials:**

- Specific stones/crystals (quartz, obsidian, jade)

- Sacred woods (cedar, oak, rowan)

- Herbs and resins (sage, copal, frankincense)

- Precious metals (gold, silver, copper)

- Organic materials (bones, feathers, shells)

**Technological Materials:**

- Clean data (uncorrupted code, ethical AI)

- Rare computing components

- Bio-materials for techno-organic crafting

- Energy sources (batteries, capacitors with high integrity)

**Spiritual Materials:**

- Blessed water from sacred sources

- Soil from ancestral lands

- Items touched by spirits

- Relics from past Elders

### Acquiring Materials

**1. Finding/Gathering:**

- Go on expeditions to natural locations

- Explore the Threshold Gardens

- Search the Glitch Wastes (dangerous but rewarding)

- Roll Oye to find what you're looking for

**2. Trading:**

- Exchange materials with other Seekers

- Trade TCP for materials

- Offer services in exchange for materials

**3. Receiving as Gifts:**

- Elders might gift materials for good work

- Spirits might offer materials in exchange for Ebo

- Faction rewards sometimes include materials

**4. Crafting/Extracting:**

- Code-Smiths can extract clean data

- Artisans can refine raw materials

- Some Rituals produce materials as byproducts

### Material Scarcity

**Not all materials are equally available:**

**Common (always available):** Candles, incense, basic offerings, common stones

**Uncommon (require 1-2 TCP or a quest):** Sacred woods, blessed water, specific crystals, clean AI code

**Rare (require significant effort/cost):** Ancestral relics, spirit-touched objects, perfectly uncorrupted data, materials from other realms

**Unique (one-of-a-kind):** Objects with specific histories, materials from major events, gifts from major spirits

**The Guide determines what's available and how to acquire it.**

### THE IMPLEMENT CRAFTING SYSTEM

### Prerequisites for Crafting

To craft a Sacred Implement, you need:

1.  **The Knowledge:** Must know the Ritual of Imbue Spirit or Ritual of the Spirit Forge

2.  **The Materials:** Specific components for what you're making

3.  **The Time:** Days or weeks of focused work

4.  **The Spirit:** A willing spirit to inhabit the Implement (or spiritual intention to charge it)

### The Crafting Process

### Step 1: Design

- Decide what you're making and what it should do

- Work with Guide to determine if it's balanced/appropriate

- Identify required materials

### Step 2: Gather Materials

- Acquire everything needed (see Material Resources above)

- May require side quests or TCP

### Step 3: Perform the Work

- Physical crafting (if physical object)

- Code writing (if digital object)

- Energetic shaping (if purely spiritual)

- This takes time: 1 week to 1 month of game time depending on complexity

### Step 4: Perform the Ritual

- Ritual of Imbue Spirit (standard power)

- Ritual of the Spirit Forge (enhanced power, requires Artisan/Code-Smith)

- Roll as normal: 10+, 7-9, or 6-

### Step 5: Outcome

**On 10+:**

- Implement works perfectly

- Has the intended properties

- Spirit is content and cooperative

**On 7-9:**

- Implement works but with a quirk or limitation

- Spirit is temperamental or demands regular offerings

- Properties are weaker than intended

**On 6-:**

- Implement doesn't work, or works wrong

- Spirit refuses to inhabit it or becomes hostile

- Materials may be partially consumed

### Sample Crafting Projects

**Simple Implement (1 week, common materials):**

- Prayer beads that help with meditation (+1 to Ebo rolls involving meditation)

- Lucky charm that boosts one specific Virtue once per session

- Basic protective sigil

**Standard Implement (2-3 weeks, uncommon materials, 5 TCP worth of resources):**

- Any Implement from the catalog in Chapter 16

- Weapon that can harm spiritual entities

- Data-core that houses a helpful AI spirit

**Complex Implement (1 month, rare materials, 10-15 TCP worth of resources, must have 5+ Breakthroughs):**

- Implement with multiple functions

- Very powerful protective or offensive tool

- Implement that grants new abilities not normally available

**Masterwork Implement (2-3 months, unique materials, 20+ TCP, must have 10+ Breakthroughs):**

- One-of-a-kind Implement of legendary power

- Creates new possibilities in the game

- Requires Elder supervision and approval

### THE EXTERNAL ECONOMY

### Money in the Outside World

Outside the Temple, regular money exists (dollars, cryptocurrency, etc.).

Seekers have limited interaction with it:

- Living expenses are covered by the Temple (food, shelter, basic clothing)

- Seekers don't have traditional jobs (they're full-time students/practitioners)

- But they might need money for:

  - Personal items

  - Gifts for family outside the Temple

  - Special materials not available at the Temple

  - Travel expenses

### Earning Money Outside

If Seekers need external money:

### Option 1: Temple Stipend

- The Temple provides small monthly stipend (enough for basics)

- ~\$200-500/month depending on seniority

### Option 2: Freelance Work

- Seekers can take outside jobs during free time

- Code-Smiths can do programming contracts

- Artisans can sell crafts

- Teachers can tutor

- Must balance with Temple commitments (Elders will notice if you're never around)

### Option 3: Family Support

- Some Seekers have family who help financially

- Not everyone does; this can create tension

**Money is not a major focus of the game,** but it exists for verisimilitude.

### BARTERING AND GIFTS

### The Gift Economy

Much of Temple economy is **gift-based**:

**How it works:**

- You make something useful and give it freely

- Others remember your generosity

- When you need something, they reciprocate

**Example:**

- Maya creates a meditation app and shares it freely

- Weeks later, she needs help debugging a corrupted spirit

- Kael (who uses her app daily) offers to help without being asked

- No TCP exchanged, just mutual support

**This is how healthy communities work.**

### Bartering

Direct exchanges also happen:

**Common trades:**

- "I'll teach you this Ritual if you help me with my research"

- "I'll craft you a Sacred Implement if you gather materials for me"

- "I'll watch your shrine while you're on a mission if you meditate with me weekly"

**No standard exchange rates.** People negotiate based on relationship, need, and fairness.

### MANAGING THE ECONOMY AS GUIDE

### Keep It Simple

**Don't make this complicated.**

Most sessions won't involve economic transactions. Players do things, earn TCP organically, spend it when needed.

**Only track what matters:**

- TCP if the group likes mechanical tracking

- Spirit Favors if they're building spirit relationships

- Major material resources (rare items only)

**Don't track:**

- Food (assumed available)

- Basic clothing (assumed available)

- Common ritual components (candles, water, etc.)

### When to Use Economics

**Use economics when:**

- Seekers want to craft major Implements

- They need access to restricted resources

- You want to create interesting choices (spend TCP on training vs. materials)

- Faction reputation feels too abstract (TCP makes it concrete)

**Don't use economics when:**

- It slows the game down

- Players aren't interested

- The focus should be on spiritual/emotional journey

### Wealth Inequality

**Be mindful:** Some players hoard TCP, others spend freely.

**Solutions if this becomes a problem:**

- Cap maximum TCP holdings

- Create "use it or lose it" situations

- Reward generosity (giving TCP away earns faction reputation)

- Don't make everything cost TCP

### OPTIONAL RULE: SHARED RESOURCES

For groups that want to emphasize community:

**All TCP goes into a shared pool.**

- No individual ownership

- Group decides together how to spend

- Requires consensus or voting

- More realistic to communal living

- Can create interesting discussions

**Pros:** Reinforces community values, prevents hoarding  
**Cons:** Can slow decisions, some players feel less agency

**Try it if your group values collective play.**

## CHAPTER 28: ADVANCED PROGRESSION

**The Path of Mastery**

### Beyond the Basics

Most Seekers complete 3-5 Breakthroughs in their first year at the Temple.

But the path doesn't end there.

**This chapter covers:**

- Mastery milestones (5th, 10th, 15th Breakthroughs)

- Becoming an Elder

- Creating new Rituals and Attunements

- Teaching others

- Legacy creation

- Retirement and Transcendence

- Becoming an Ancestor

### THE MASTERY MILESTONES

**5th Breakthrough: ADEPT**

**What This Means:**

You're no longer a beginner. You understand the fundamentals and have proven your commitment.

**Mechanical Benefits:**

- May increase one Virtue by +1 (see Virtue increases below)

- Access to harder Mysteries (6-8 slice Clocks)

- Can begin teaching junior Seekers

- Eligibility for faction leadership roles

**Social Recognition:**

- Other Seekers respect you

- Elders take you seriously

- You're considered competent

**Responsibilities:**

- Expected to mentor at least one junior Seeker

- May be called upon to help with Temple duties

- Your mistakes have greater consequences (you should know better)

**Narrative Shift:** The Guide should start treating you as capable. Challenges should be more complex, stakes higher, consequences more significant.

**10th Breakthrough: ELDER-IN-TRAINING**

**What This Means:**

You're being groomed for leadership. The Temple sees you as a future Elder.

**Mechanical Benefits:**

- May increase one Virtue by +1 (second increase)

- Access to the hardest Mysteries (8-slice Clocks)

- Can propose new Rituals for approval (see Creating Rituals below)

- Eligibility for Council of Elders observation

**Social Recognition:**

- Called "Elder-in-Training \[Name\]" formally

- Given significant responsibilities

- Your voice carries weight in decisions

**Responsibilities:**

- Expected to teach regularly

- May be assigned apprentices

- Represent the Temple in external matters

- Help resolve community conflicts

**Narrative Shift:** You're transitioning from "doing the work" to "guiding others in the work." Mysteries should increasingly involve leadership, teaching, and responsibility for others.

**15th Breakthrough: ELDER**

**What This Means:**

Full Elder status. You're now a spiritual authority and Temple leader.

**Mechanical Benefits:**

- May increase one Virtue by +1 (final increase possible)

- Can create new Rituals without approval (within reason)

- Can teach any Ritual you know

- Access to restricted knowledge and locations

- Eligible for faction leadership positions

- May take formal apprentices

- Voice on the Council of Elders

**Social Recognition:**

- Addressed as "Elder \[Name\]"

- Your quarters in the Elder's section

- Younger Seekers seek your guidance

- Your decisions shape Temple policy

**Responsibilities:**

- Mentor multiple Seekers

- Serve on at least one Council committee

- Help govern the Temple

- Make difficult decisions that affect many people

- Model Iwa-pele at all times (the weight of representation)

**Narrative Shift:** You are now one of the authority figures. Much of your play involves teaching, leading, resolving complex ethical situations, and shaping the Temple's future.

### VIRTUE INCREASES

### The Three Increases

At your 5th, 10th, and 15th Breakthroughs, you may increase one Virtue by +1.

**Rules:**

- No Virtue can exceed +3 (maximum mastery)

- Choose carefully (this is permanent)

- Represents fundamental growth, not just skill gain

**Strategic Choices:**

### Option 1: Strengthen Your Strength

- Take your +2 Virtue to +3

- Become exceptional at what you're already good at

- Max out early (could have +3 by 10th Breakthrough)

### Option 2: Shore Up Weakness

- Raise your +0 or +1 Virtues

- Become more well-rounded

- Takes longer to maximize any one Virtue

### Option 3: Follow the Story

- Raise whatever Virtue the story suggests

- If you've been facing Foriti Lessons constantly, maybe that's the one growing

**Most Seekers end up with:** +3, +2, +2, +1 or +3, +3, +1, +1 by Elder status

### CREATING NEW RITUALS

### When You Can Create Rituals

**10th Breakthrough:** Can propose new Rituals for Elder approval  
**15th Breakthrough:** Can create new Rituals freely (still subject to peer review)

### The Creation Process

**Step 1: Identify the Need** What does this Ritual do that existing Rituals don't?

**Step 2: Design the Ritual** Define:

- **Name**

- **Ebo Required:** What must be offered/done?

- **Virtue Tested:** Which Virtue does this test?

- **Effect (10+, 7-9, 6-):** What happens at each outcome?

- **Prerequisites** (if any): Who can learn this?

**Step 3: Test It** Perform the Ritual yourself at least three times. Document results. Refine based on outcomes.

**Step 4: Teach It** If you're an Elder, teach it to at least two others. Verify they can perform it successfully.

**Step 5: Add to the Hymnal** Submit it for inclusion in the Great Hymnal. The Council of Elders reviews. If approved, it becomes Temple canon.

### Guidelines for Ritual Creation

**New Rituals should:**

- Fill a genuine gap in the existing Hymnal

- Be balanced (not more powerful than similar Rituals)

- Respect the source traditions

- Have clear, meaningful Ebo requirements

- Create interesting choices at 7-9 and 6-

**New Rituals should NOT:**

- Trivialize existing challenges

- Allow "cheat codes" or shortcuts

- Violate core principles (no harming innocents, no forcing unwilling participants, etc.)

- Be purely mechanical with no spiritual depth

**Work with your Guide to ensure balance.**

### TEACHING OTHERS

### When You Start Teaching

**5th Breakthrough:** Can mentor individual Seekers informally  
**10th Breakthrough:** Can teach classes and formal apprenticeships  
**15th Breakthrough:** Can teach any Ritual you know to qualified students

### The Teaching Responsibility

Teaching is not optional at advanced levels. **Elders teach.**

### Why?

- Knowledge must be passed on

- Teaching deepens your own understanding

- Service to community is core to Iwa-pele

- The Temple depends on knowledge transmission

**What You Teach:**

- Rituals you know

- Attunements you have

- Philosophical insights from your journey

- Practical skills related to your Archetype

- How to navigate specific challenges

### Mechanics of Teaching

**Informal Mentoring:**

- Advising a Seeker on their Mysteries

- Answering questions

- Modeling good practice

- No formal roll needed

**Formal Teaching (Rituals, Attunements, Complex Skills):**

- Requires dedicated time (weeks to months)

- Perform the Ritual of Wisdom Transfer (from Hymnal of Knowing), or

- Teach through sustained instruction and practice

- Student must meet prerequisites

- Roll to see if transmission succeeds (use Oye or Iwa)

**On 10+:** Student learns successfully  
**On 7-9:** Student learns but struggle remains, or both teacher and student gain 1 Imbalance from difficulty  
**On 6-:** Student doesn't grasp it yet, both gain Imbalance, must try again with different approach

### Taking Apprentices

**At 10th Breakthrough,** you can take formal apprentices.

**Apprentice Relationship:**

- 1-3 apprentices at a time (more is too many)

- Commit to their full training (years)

- They learn from you specifically, not the Temple generally

- Deep relationship: you're responsible for their growth

- When they achieve their 5th Breakthrough, the apprenticeship is complete

**Benefits to Teacher:**

- Fulfills Elder responsibilities

- Deepens your own practice through teaching

- Builds legacy

- Creates lasting bonds

**Benefits to Apprentice:**

- Personal attention from a master

- Faster learning

- Connection to teacher's spirit allies and knowledge

- Belonging

### LEGACY CREATION

### What Is Legacy?

**Legacy** is what you leave behind that persists after you're gone.

At advanced levels, Seekers think about legacy intentionally.

**Types of Legacy:**

**Knowledge Legacy:**

- Rituals you created

- Texts you wrote

- Archive contributions

- Teaching lineages

**Physical Legacy:**

- Sacred Implements you crafted

- Buildings or spaces you created/consecrated

- Gardens you planted

- Art you made

**Community Legacy:**

- Seekers you trained

- Policies you shaped

- Conflicts you resolved

- Communities you built

**Spiritual Legacy:**

- Your embodiment of Iwa-pele

- Example you set

- Wisdom you modeled

- How you faced your death

### The Ritual of the Legacy Stone

(See Chapter 15 - Hymnal of Weaving)

**At 10+ Breakthroughs,** you may create a Legacy Stone—an object containing your essential wisdom for future generations.

**This is profound work:** Distilling your life's teachings into transmittable form.

**If successful:** Future Seekers can access your wisdom even after your death.

**This is how Elders become Ancestors while still living.**

### RETIREMENT AND TRANSCENDENCE

### When to Retire

Some Elders never retire—they teach until they die.

Others step back after 20+ years of service.

**Retirement means:**

- Reducing teaching load

- Stepping down from Council duties

- Focusing on personal practice

- Preparing for death

**You're still an Elder,** but semi-active.

**Benefits:**

- More time for deep practice

- Less stress and responsibility

- Space to contemplate your path

- Preparation for transition

### Transcendence

**Transcendence** is choosing how you leave the physical world.

**Three Paths:**

### 1. Natural Death

- Allow death to come when it comes

- Face it with equanimity

- Trust the process

- No ritual needed

### 2. Conscious Dying

- The Ritual of the Death Journey (Chapter 14)

- Cross into the ancestor-realm while still alive

- Say goodbyes

- Choose the moment of departure

- Requires tremendous Foriti and preparation

### 3. Spirit Transformation

- Extremely rare

- Your physical body dies but your consciousness fully transitions to spirit form

- You become a permanent spirit-ally of the Temple

- Requires 20+ Breakthroughs and mastery of threshold work

- Only a handful have succeeded in Temple history

**All three are honored.**

There is no "right" way to die.

### BECOMING AN ANCESTOR

### After Death

When an Elder dies, their relationship with the Temple changes but doesn't end.

**If they prepared:**

- Legacy Stone contains their wisdom

- Apprentices carry their teachings

- Spirit may remain accessible for consultation

**Ancestral Rituals:**

- Memorial ceremony (honoring their life and work)

- Placement in the Crypts of Memory

- Regular offerings at their shrine

- Invocation when their wisdom is needed

**Mechanical Effect:** Seekers can perform the Ritual of the Ancestral Call to contact deceased Elders specifically.

**The Elder-as-Ancestor can:**

- Answer questions

- Provide guidance

- Warn of dangers

- Bless endeavors

- Continue teaching from beyond

**But:** Ancestors are not servants. They choose whether to answer. They should be respected, not commanded.

### ENDGAME CONTENT

### For 20+ Breakthrough Seekers

If a campaign runs long enough that Seekers achieve 20+ Breakthroughs:

**Possible Endgame Content:**

### 1. Major Temple Crises

- The sealed AI entity beneath the Temple awakens

- External threat to the Temple's existence

- Fundamental philosophical schism threatens to split the community

- Corruption at the highest levels of leadership

### 2. Creating New Lineages

- Discover or establish new spiritual lineages

- Integrate new traditions into the Temple

- Expand the Temple's reach

### 3. Founding New Temples

- Establish satellite Temples in other locations

- Train the first generation of Seekers elsewhere

- Spread the practice globally

### 4. Transcendence Quests

- Attempt conscious dying or spirit transformation

- Guide an entire generation through Transcendence

- Unlock new thresholds to unexplored realms

### 5. Becoming Legend

- Your deeds are taught to new Seekers

- Rituals are named after you

- You become a mythic figure while still living

### FINAL THOUGHTS ON MASTERY

**Mastery is not about power.**

It's about:

- Deep understanding

- Consistent practice

- Service to others

- Embodiment of principles

- Wisdom earned through experience

**An Elder with 15 Breakthroughs is not "stronger" than a Seeker with 3.**

They're **more responsible, more accountable, more visible.**

**With mastery comes weight.**

The path gets harder, not easier.

**But also more meaningful.**

## CHAPTER 29: CAMPAIGNS AND LONG PLAY

**What Is a Campaign?** A **campaign** is an extended series of connected sessions telling an ongoing story.

**Short campaigns:** 6-12 sessions, 1-3 Mysteries  
**Medium campaigns:** 12-30 sessions, 3-6 Mysteries, Seekers reach 5-10 Breakthroughs  
**Long campaigns:** 30+ sessions, 6+ Mysteries, Seekers may become Elders

**This chapter teaches you how to run satisfying long-term play.**

### CAMPAIGN STRUCTURES

### Structure 1: The Episodic Campaign

**How it works:**

- Each Mystery is self-contained

- Mysteries connect loosely (same characters, same world, but not one continuous plot)

- Seekers can drop in/out without disrupting narrative

- Like a TV series with standalone episodes

**Advantages:**

- Flexible scheduling

- Low pressure

- Easy for new players to join

- Less prep between sessions

**Disadvantages:**

- Can feel disconnected

- Harder to build momentum

- Character arcs develop slowly

**Best for:** Casual groups, irregular schedules, players who prefer variety

### Structure 2: The Serial Campaign

**How it works:**

- Mysteries build on each other

- Each Mystery reveals pieces of a larger story

- Cliffhangers and callbacks

- Must play in order

- Like a TV series with continuous plot

**Advantages:**

- Deep investment

- Satisfying reveals and payoffs

- Complex storylines possible

- Strong narrative momentum

**Disadvantages:**

- Requires consistent attendance

- More prep

- Missing sessions creates gaps

- Pressure to "get it right"

**Best for:** Committed groups, regular schedules, players who love deep stories

### Structure 3: The Hybrid Campaign

**How it works:**

- Mix of standalone and connected Mysteries

- Some sessions advance the main plot

- Some sessions are side stories

- Flexible but cohesive

**Advantages:**

- Best of both approaches

- Flexibility when needed

- Depth where it matters

- Easy to adjust to group energy

**Disadvantages:**

- Requires careful balance

- Can confuse players about what's "important"

- More Guide skill needed

**Best for:** Most groups (this is the recommended default)

### BUILDING A CAMPAIGN ARC

### The Three-Act Structure

### Act One: Establishment (Sessions 1-6)

**Goals:**

- Introduce the world and NPCs

- Establish character relationships

- Set up mysteries and questions

- Complete 1-2 Breakthroughs

**Narrative focus:**

- Learning the Temple

- Forming bonds

- First challenges

- Building investment

**Pacing:** Slower, exploratory

### Act Two: Complication (Sessions 7-20)

**Goals:**

- Deepen conflicts

- Raise stakes

- Character growth and change

- Complete 3-7 Breakthroughs

**Narrative focus:**

- Harder challenges

- Faction involvement

- Personal Crossroads moments

- Secrets revealed

**Pacing:** Momentum building, occasional breathers

### Act Three: Resolution (Sessions 21-30)

**Goals:**

- Resolve main conflicts

- Character culminations

- Major world changes

- Reach 8-12 Breakthroughs (or more)

**Narrative focus:**

- Climactic confrontations

- Legacy decisions

- Consequences of earlier choices

- Satisfying endings (or new beginnings)

**Pacing:** Intense with cathartic moments

### The Spine: Your Central Question

Every campaign should have a **central question** that everything connects to.

**Examples:**

**"Can the factions unite before the Temple falls?"**

- Mysteries involve faction conflicts

- NPCs take sides

- Seekers must navigate politics

- Resolution: united Temple or permanent schism

**"What is the truth about the Founder's secret?"**

- Mysteries reveal pieces of history

- Spirits have conflicting accounts

- Elders hide or reveal information

- Resolution: the truth discovered and consequences faced

**"Will the AI entity beneath the Temple be freed or destroyed?"**

- Mysteries involve the entity's influence

- Spirits and Seekers take different positions

- Seekers must decide what's right

- Resolution: entity's fate determined

**Your central question should:**

- Be answerable but complex

- Divide opinions (no obvious "right" answer)

- Touch multiple characters

- Build to satisfying conclusion

### WEAVING MULTIPLE MYSTERIES

### The Primary-Secondary Structure

At any time, the group has:

**1 Primary Mystery (Main Focus)**

- This is what the current sessions center on

- Most play time goes here

- Clock is actively filling

### 1-2 Secondary Mysteries (Background)

- These exist but aren't urgent

- Occasional scenes advance them

- Can become Primary later

### Personal Mysteries (Individual)

- Each Seeker has personal goals/struggles

- These weave through both Primary and Secondary

- Don't need formal Clocks

**Example:**

*Primary: "The Data Plague" (6-slice) - A virus is corrupting spirits  
Secondary: "Faction Tensions" (8-slice) - Factions disagree about how to handle plague  
Personal: Maya wants to prove herself to Elder Amaka*

Sessions advance all three, but most focus goes to the Plague.

When Plague resolves, Faction Tensions becomes Primary.

### Callbacks and Setup

**Callbacks** make long campaigns satisfying.

**How to create them:**

**Session 5:** Seekers meet a minor NPC spirit, the Wandering Data-Echo  
**Session 12:** That spirit appears again, mentions it saw something strange  
**Session 18:** That "something strange" becomes a major plot point

**Players feel:** "Oh wow, that connects back to Session 5!"

**This requires:**

- Note-taking (Guide should track NPCs, events, details)

- Seeding early (introduce things before they matter)

- Payoff later (bring them back meaningfully)

**Rule of Three:** Introduce something three times for maximum impact.

1.  First mention: "Huh, interesting"

2.  Second mention: "Wait, that again?"

3.  Third mention: "This is important!"

### CHARACTER ARCS IN CAMPAIGNS

### Personal Growth Over Time

Each Seeker should have a **character arc**—a journey from who they are to who they become.

**Arc Elements:**

**Starting State:** Who are you at campaign beginning?  
**Core Struggle:** What internal conflict defines you?  
**Breakthrough Moments:** When do you change?  
**Ending State:** Who have you become?

### Example: Maya's Arc

**Starting:** Insecure Seer who doubts her visions  
**Struggle:** Trusts others more than herself  
**Breakthroughs:**

- Session 8: Vision saves the group, starts trusting herself

- Session 15: Stands up to Elder Amaka when her vision contradicts Archive

- Session 22: Fully confident, teaches other Seers

**Ending:** Recognized visionary who trusts her sight

### Supporting Character Arcs

**As Guide:**

**Notice what the player is exploring:**

- Do they keep choosing Iwa Lessons? They're interested in integrity

- Do they avoid Foriti Lessons? Maybe that's the growth edge

- Do they talk about a specific NPC constantly? Build that relationship

**Create Lessons that test the arc:**

- If Maya's arc is learning to trust herself, give her situations where trusting others fails

- If Diego's arc is learning boundaries, present people who take advantage of his kindness

**Reward growth:**

- When they demonstrate change, notice it

- "Maya, you're not asking for validation anymore. You just stated your vision as fact. That's growth."

- Offer Breakthrough rewards that reflect their arc

### WORLD EVENTS AND TIME

### The Living World

The Temple world continues even when Seekers aren't the focus.

**Between sessions:**

- NPCs have their own stories

- Factions make moves

- Spirits act on their agendas

- The world changes

**Show this through:**

**Rumors and news:** "You hear that the Vigil discovered something big in the Archive."

**Changed circumstances:** "When you return from your mission, the East Garden is now restricted. Wardens everywhere."

**NPC updates:** "Yara passed her 5th Breakthrough while you were gone. She's different now."

**This makes the world feel real.**

### Major Temple Events

Plan 2-3 **major events** per campaign:

**Examples:**

**The Festival of Ancestors** (planned celebration)

- Everyone participates

- Chance for character moments

- Introduce new NPCs

- Advance secondary plots

**The Network Crash** (crisis event)

- Digital spirits go silent

- Panic at the Temple

- Forces immediate response

- Tests group under pressure

**An Elder's Death** (transformative event)

- Community mourns

- Power dynamics shift

- Funeral becomes major scene

- Legacy questions arise

**Major events should:**

- Involve everyone

- Create lasting consequences

- Offer character growth opportunities

- Be memorable

### PACING THE CAMPAIGN

### Session Rhythm

**Not every session should be intense.**

**Vary the rhythm:**

**High-Intensity Sessions (30%):**

- Crossroads moments

- Major confrontations

- Dangerous missions

- Climactic reveals

**Medium Sessions (50%):**

- Investigation and problem-solving

- Social interaction and politics

- Ritual work and Ebo

- Steady Mystery progress

**Low-Intensity Sessions (20%):**

- Character development and relationships

- Teaching and learning

- Community building

- Quiet reflection

**Example 6-session arc:**

- Session 1: Medium (introduce Mystery)

- Session 2: Medium (investigate)

- Session 3: Low (character development)

- Session 4: High (major confrontation)

- Session 5: Medium (deal with aftermath)

- Session 6: Low/High mix (resolution and Breakthrough)

### The Five-Session Structure

If unsure how to pace, use this:

**Session 1:** Hook (introduce the Mystery)  
**Session 2:** Investigate (gather information, build understanding)  
**Session 3:** Complication (things get harder, stakes raise)  
**Session 4:** Crisis (Crossroads moment, major challenge)  
**Session 5:** Resolution (Mystery completes, Breakthrough happens)

Then breathe before starting the next Mystery.

### ENDING A CAMPAIGN

### When to End

**Signs it's time:**

- Main story questions answered

- Characters have completed their arcs

- Group energy is flagging

- Scheduling becomes impossible

- It feels complete

**Don't drag it out.** Better to end strong than fade away.

### The Final Session

**Plan the final session carefully:**

**Include:**

### 1. Resolution of main conflict/question

- Answer the central campaign question

- Resolve the biggest Mystery

### 2. Character epilogues

- What happens to each Seeker after?

- Where do they go?

- What do they become?

### 3. World state

- How has the Temple changed?

- What will future generations inherit?

### 4. Reflection

- Out-of-character: what did we learn?

- What were favorite moments?

- What will we remember?

### 5. Gratitude

- Thank the players

- Thank each character

- Honor the journey

### Three Types of Endings

**The Continuation Ending:**

- Story could continue but we're stopping here

- "And their journey goes on..."

- Leaves things open

- Good if you might return to this campaign

**The Resolution Ending:**

- All major questions answered

- Arcs complete

- Satisfying closure

- Good for planned endings

**The Transcendence Ending:**

- Major transformation

- Characters become Elders/Ancestors/Legends

- The world fundamentally changes

- Good for epic campaigns

**All are valid. Choose what fits your story.**

### CAMPAIGN MAINTENANCE

### Between Sessions

**As Guide, maintain momentum:**

**Take notes:**

- What happened

- What was awesome

- What needs follow-up

- NPC status updates

**Prep next session:**

- Review current Mysteries

- Plan 2-3 scenes

- Consider pacing

- Prep any new NPCs/spirits

**Communicate with players:**

- Send session recap (optional)

- Ask for feedback

- Remind of next session

- Share anything they should prepare

**Rest:**

- Don't burn out

- It's okay to take breaks

- The game should be fun for you too

### Player Turnover

**If a player leaves:**

**Option 1:** Their character becomes an NPC  
**Option 2:** Write them out narratively (they go on a long journey, they transition to another Temple, they retire)  
**Option 3:** New player takes over the character  
**Option 4:** Character dies (only if player agrees)

**If a new player joins:**

**Option 1:** They join as a new Seeker arriving at the Temple  
**Option 2:** They were always there but in the background  
**Option 3:** They transfer from another location

**Give them on-boarding scenes to integrate.**

### FINAL CAMPAIGN ADVICE

**Stay flexible.** Your plan will change. That's good.

**Listen to players.** They'll tell you what they want (through what excites them).

**Trust the process.** Not every session will be perfect. The campaign is greater than any single session.

**Take breaks.** Long campaigns need pauses. Schedule them.

**Celebrate milestones.** When someone hits 5th Breakthrough, make it special. When you hit Session 20, acknowledge it.

**Let it breathe.** Don't force every moment to be meaningful. Sometimes you're just hanging out in-character. That's valuable too.

**End well.** Even if the campaign didn't go as planned, give it a good ending. Your players deserve that.
