# BOOK SEVEN: RESOURCES

## CHAPTER 30: SAFETY AND SESSION ZERO

### Creating Sacred Space

### Why Safety Matters

Temple of Gu deals with **heavy themes:**

- Spiritual crisis and doubt

- Fear and trauma

- Moral failure and ego

- Death and grief

- Psychological struggle

**This is by design.** The game is about growth through challenge.

**But:** Not everyone wants to engage with every theme. Not every table is ready for every type of content.

**Safety tools help you:**

- Set boundaries before problems arise

- Navigate difficult content respectfully

- Pause when someone's overwhelmed

- Build trust in your group

**This is not optional.** If you skip safety tools, you risk genuine harm.

### SESSION ZERO: THE ESSENTIAL FOUNDATION

### What Is Session Zero?

**Session Zero** is a pre-game session where you:

- Don't play the game yet

- Discuss expectations, boundaries, and safety

- Create characters together

- Establish group agreements

- Build excitement

**Time needed:** 2-3 hours

**Who attends:** Everyone who will play (all players + Guide)

### THE SESSION ZERO AGENDA

### Part 1: Introductions and Expectations (30 minutes)

**Go around the table:**

**Everyone shares:**

1.  Name and pronouns

2.  Experience with TTRPGs (veteran or new?)

3.  What excites them about this game

4.  What makes them nervous about this game

**Guide shares:**

- Your experience as Guide

- What kind of game you want to run (tone, themes)

- Your hopes for the group

**This builds connection and surfaces concerns early.**

### Part 2: Safety Tools (45 minutes)

**Introduce and agree on safety tools.** (See next section for details)

Minimum tools to use:

1.  **Lines and Veils**

2.  **X-Card or equivalent pause mechanism**

3.  **Debriefing protocol**

Explain each tool. Practice using them. Get explicit agreement.

### Part 3: Table Agreements (30 minutes)

**Co-create agreements about:**

**Attendance and Scheduling:**

- How often do we play?

- What if someone can't make it?

- How do we handle absences?

**Communication:**

- How do we communicate between sessions? (group chat, email?)

- How do we give feedback?

- What if someone's uncomfortable with something?

**Conflict:**

- How do we handle disagreements?

- In-character conflict vs. out-of-character conflict?

- What's acceptable and what's not?

**Engagement:**

- Phones/devices at the table?

- Note-taking expectations?

- Staying present?

**Write these down.** Revisit them regularly.

### Part 4: Character Creation (60 minutes)

**Create characters together:**

- Use Chapter 5 (Creating Your Seeker)

- Help each other make choices

- Establish relationships between characters

- Share backstories

**Guide facilitates:**

- Answer questions

- Ensure diversity of Archetypes/Lineages

- Help balance Virtue spreads

- Build connections

### Part 5: Closing (15 minutes)

**Wrap up:**

- Preview what Session 1 will be

- Set next session date

- Express excitement

- Thank everyone for showing up

### SAFETY TOOLS IN DETAIL

### Tool 1: LINES AND VEILS

**Lines:** Content that will not appear in the game at all  
**Veils:** Content that can exist but happens "off-screen"

**How to use:**

**Before the game, ask everyone:** "What topics are Lines for you? What topics are Veils?"

**Common Lines:**

- Sexual violence

- Harm to children

- Animal cruelty

- Specific phobias (spiders, drowning, etc.)

- Eating disorders

- Self-harm

- Specific trauma triggers

**Common Veils:**

- Sex (it can happen but we fade to black)

- Torture (we know it occurred but don't describe details)

- Severe violence (acknowledge it without graphic description)

**Write down all Lines.** Guide must honor them absolutely.

**Veils are flexible** but respect them.

**Update Lines/Veils** as needed. Things change.

### Tool 2: THE X-CARD

### Created by John Stavropoulos

**How it works:**

Place an actual card with an X on it in the middle of the table (or in digital space if playing online).

**Anyone can tap it at any time for any reason, no explanation needed.**

**When someone taps the X:**

1.  **Stop immediately**

2.  **Rewind or skip forward**

3.  **Don't ask why** (they don't have to explain)

4.  **Continue playing**

**Example:**

*Guide: "The spirit manifests as a swarm of spiders covering—"  
Player: \[taps X-Card\]  
Guide: "—actually, the spirit manifests as a cloud of shadow. Same mechanical effect, different imagery. Continuing..."*

**No discussion. No justification needed.**

**This removes shame and makes safety accessible.**

### Tool 3: SCRIPT CHANGE

### Created by Beau Jágr Sheldon

**More nuanced than X-Card:**

Like a remote control, you can:

- **Rewind:** Go back and change what just happened

- **Fast Forward:** Skip this scene entirely

- **Pause:** Stop and discuss out-of-character

- **Resume:** Continue after a pause

**How to use:**

Anyone can call these out loud:

- "I want to rewind that."

- "Can we fast forward through this?"

- "Pause—I need to check in."

**Useful for longer, more complex safety needs.**

### Tool 4: STARS AND WISHES

### Created by Lu Quade

**Use at the end of each session:**

**Stars:** What went well? What did you love?  
**Wishes:** What do you hope to see more (or less) of?

**Go around the circle, everyone shares:**

"My star is the scene where Maya confronted the Elder. So powerful."  
"My wish is for more quiet character moments, less constant crisis."

**This creates regular feedback loop without confrontation.**

### Tool 5: SUPPORT FLOWERS / COMFORT OBJECTS

**For in-person play:**

Provide stress toys, fidgets, candy, or soft objects players can hold when scenes get intense.

**This helps:**

- Physical grounding

- Self-soothing

- Non-verbal signal of discomfort (if someone's squeezing the stress ball hard, maybe check in)

### CONTENT WARNINGS

### How to Give Content Warnings

**Before a potentially difficult scene:**

**Guide says:** "Content warning: The next scene involves \[specific content\]. Everyone okay to proceed?"

**Wait for explicit agreement.**

**If anyone says no or hesitates:**

- Use a Veil instead

- Skip the scene

- Change the approach

**Example:**

*"Content warning: This Crossroads is going to involve Maya confronting her fear of abandonment. It might get emotionally heavy. Everyone good with that?"*

**Give players agency to opt out without shame.**

### DEBRIEF PROTOCOLS

### After Intense Sessions

**Always debrief after heavy content:**

**Ask:**

- "How is everyone feeling?"

- "Do we need to talk about anything?"

- "What support do people need?"

**Allow:**

- Silence (people processing)

- Emotions (it's okay to cry)

- Space (someone might need to step away)

**Offer:**

- Hugs or physical comfort (if welcome)

- Continued conversation

- Resources if needed

**Don't force it.** Some people need time alone to process.

### RECOGNIZING PROBLEMS

### Warning Signs Someone's Not Okay

**Watch for:**

- Sudden withdrawal or silence

- Visible distress (tears, shaking, hyperventilating)

- Dissociation (thousand-yard stare, not responding)

- Anger or defensive reactions

- Leaving abruptly

**If you see this:**

**Stop immediately.** Call a pause.

**Check in directly:** "Hey, are you okay? Do you need a break?"

**Offer options:**

- Step outside for air

- End the session early

- Talk about it or not talk about it

- Continue or not continue

**Respect their choice.**

### HANDLING BOUNDARY VIOLATIONS

### If Someone Crosses a Line

**It will happen eventually.** Accidents occur even with good intentions.

**Response:**

### 1. Stop the content immediately

**2. Acknowledge:**  
"I crossed a Line. I'm sorry."

**3. Check in:**  
"Are you okay? What do you need?"

**4. Adjust:**  
Change the content, offer alternatives, respect their wishes

**5. Learn:**  
Update your understanding, don't make the same mistake twice

**6. Move on:**  
Once addressed, continue playing (if everyone's ready)

### If Someone Repeatedly Violates Boundaries

**This is different from accidents.**

**Pattern of:**

- Ignoring Lines/Veils

- Dismissing X-Card

- Pushing boundaries "as a joke"

- Making others uncomfortable deliberately

**Response:**

**1. Private conversation:**  
"This behavior isn't okay. It needs to stop."

**2. Set clear consequence:**  
"If this continues, you can't play with us anymore."

**3. Follow through:**  
If it continues, remove them from the group

**Your first responsibility is to the people being harmed, not the person causing harm.**

### SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS

### Playing with Trauma Survivors

**Many people have trauma.** RPGs can be therapeutic but also triggering.

**Best practices:**

- Be extra careful with safety tools

- Don't assume you know someone's triggers

- Respect that triggers can be unexpected

- Support without fixing

- Professional therapy exists for a reason (RPGs aren't therapy)

### Playing with Minors

**If anyone at the table is under 18:**

**Additional safety:**

- Get parent/guardian consent

- Be extra cautious with mature content

- Default to Veils for anything questionable

- Have another adult present if possible

- Know mandatory reporting laws in your area

### Playing Online

**Digital safety:**

- Video when possible (easier to read body language)

- Private chat option for discreet communication

- Virtual X-Card (emoji or phrase in chat)

- Tech problems are okay (they're not safety failures)

- Check in more frequently (harder to read people)

### ONGOING SAFETY MAINTENANCE

**Safety isn't one-and-done.**

**Regular check-ins:**

- Monthly: "Any Lines/Veils to add or change?"

- Every 5 sessions: "How's everyone feeling about the game?"

- After major events: "That was intense. Everyone okay?"

**Update agreements as needed.** What worked in Session 1 might not work in Session 20.

**Model vulnerability.** Guide should share when they're unsure or uncomfortable too.

**Normalize safety talk.** The more you use safety tools, the less weird it feels.

### WHEN TO SEEK OUTSIDE HELP

**RPGs can surface real issues:**

- Trauma responses

- Mental health crises

- Relationship problems

- Addiction or compulsion

**If someone is struggling beyond table level:**

**Encourage professional help:**

- Therapist

- Support groups

- Crisis hotlines

- Trusted adults/mentors

**Be clear about your limits:** "I care about you, and I'm here as a friend and fellow player. But I'm not trained to help with this. Let's find someone who is."

**Resources:**

**Crisis hotlines:**

- National Suicide Prevention Lifeline (US): 988

- Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741

- SAMHSA National Helpline: 1-800-662-4357

### FINAL THOUGHTS ON SAFETY

**Safety is sacred.**

This game asks people to be vulnerable—to explore fear, doubt, ego, failure, growth.

**That's a gift they give you.**

Honor it by creating the safest space possible.

**Safety isn't weakness.** It's strength.

**Safety isn't limiting.** It's liberating.

When people feel safe, they can go deeper, take bigger risks, be more honest.

**That's where the magic happens.**

## CHAPTER 31: QUICK START GUIDE

### How to Use This Chapter

**Want to try the game immediately?** This chapter gives you everything needed for your first session:

- Core rules summary (2 pages)

- 5 pre-generated Seekers (ready to play)

- 1 ready-to-run introductory Mystery

- Step-by-step first session guide

**Time needed:** 2-3 hours for your first session

### CORE RULES SUMMARY

### The Basics

**You are Seekers** at the Temple of Gu, students walking a spiritual path.

**The Guide** presents challenges (called Lessons) and narrates the world.

**When facing a Lesson,** you roll 2d6 + one of your four Virtues:

- **Suuru (Patience):** Endurance, calm, listening

- **Oye (Insight):** Understanding, perception, research

- **Foriti (Perseverance):** Courage, pushing through, facing fear

- **Iwa (Integrity):** Honesty, service, keeping promises

**Results:**

- **10+:** Full success, no complications

- **7-9:** Success, but there's a cost or complication

- **6-:** Things get harder, you gain 1 Imbalance

### Imbalance and Balance

Your **Balance Track** has 6 boxes: \[ \] \[ \] \[ \] \[ \] \[ \] \[ \]

**Imbalance** comes in three Strains:

- **(D) Doubt:** Confusion, uncertainty

- **(F) Fear:** Terror, overwhelm

- **(E) Ego:** Pride, selfishness

Mark a box when you gain Imbalance. Note the Strain.

**When all 6 boxes fill,** you reach a **Crossroads**—a major spiritual confrontation.

**Clear Imbalance** by performing **Ebo** (sacred practice):

- Meditate, pray, serve others, create art, fast, make amends

- Roll 2d6 + appropriate Virtue

- **10+:** Clear 1 Imbalance

- **7-9:** Clear 1 but there's a complication

- **6-:** Doesn't work, try differently

### Mysteries and Breakthroughs

**Mysteries** are your quests—spiritual problems to solve.

Track progress with a **Clock** (4, 6, or 8 slices).

Fill slices by succeeding at relevant Lessons:

- **10+:** Fill 2 slices

- **7-9:** Fill 1 slice

- **6-:** Fill 0 slices

**When the Clock fills,** you complete the Mystery and earn a **Breakthrough:**

- Learn a new Ritual

- Gain a Sacred Implement

- Learn an Attunement

- Advance your Archetype Path

### That's It

**Those are the core mechanics.** Everything else builds from here.

### READY-TO-RUN INTRO MYSTERY

**"THE FORGOTTEN SHRINE" (4-slice Clock)**

**Setup:**  
An old shrine on Temple grounds has been neglected for years. Recently, strange things have been happening nearby—lights flickering, data glitching, people having unsettling dreams. An Elder asks the Seekers to investigate.

**The Truth:**  
A lonely place-spirit named Omi inhabits the shrine. It's not hostile—just forgotten, sad, and acting out for attention like a neglected child.

**The Solution:**  
Restore the shrine, acknowledge Omi, make regular offerings, promise to remember it.

### Scene 1: THE CALL (15 minutes)

**Location:** Community Hearth, morning

**Setup:**  
Elder Yuki Tanaka approaches the group during breakfast.

**Elder Yuki says:**  
"Good morning, Seekers. I have a task for you, if you're willing. There's an old shrine in the East Garden—dedicated to a water-spirit named Omi. No one's tended it in years. Recently, people near that area have been having... difficulties. I'd like you to investigate and resolve whatever's happening. Are you willing?"

**Let the Seekers:**

- Ask questions (Yuki knows the shrine exists but not much else)

- Accept or decline (assume they accept)

- Decide how to approach this

**Guide says:**  
"You head to the East Garden. The shrine is easy to find—a small stone structure near a dry stream bed. It's covered in moss and leaves. The offerings left there are ancient and rotted. The place feels... sad."

**Call for a roll:**  
"Someone roll Oye to sense the spiritual state of this place."

**On 10+:** You sense a lonely, gentle presence. Not hostile, just forgotten and hurting. Fill 2 slices.

**On 7-9:** You sense loneliness, but also flickers of anger. Fill 1 slice. Mark 1 Imbalance (Doubt) as you're not sure if it's safe.

**On 6-:** The place feels chaotic. You can't get a read. Mark 1 Imbalance (Doubt). Fill 0 slices.

### Scene 2: MEETING OMI (20 minutes)

**Setup:**  
The spirit manifests—a shimmering figure of water and light, vaguely humanoid, constantly shifting.

**Omi says (softly, sadly):**  
"You... you came? No one comes anymore. No one remembers. Are you here to hurt me? To tell me to leave?"

**Let the Seekers:**

- Talk to Omi

- Ask what it needs

- Offer comfort or investigation

**What Omi wants:**

- To be remembered and acknowledged

- Offerings and care

- Proof that someone will keep visiting

**Call for a roll:**  
"To calm Omi and understand what it really needs, someone roll Suuru."

**On 10+:** Omi opens up completely. Tells you its history (was tended by an Elder who died 30 years ago, has been alone since). Fill 2 slices. (Clock should be full or close now.)

**On 7-9:** Omi tells you but starts crying (water manifesting). The grief is overwhelming. Mark 1 Imbalance (Fear) from witnessing its pain. Fill 1 slice.

**On 6-:** Omi's emotions overwhelm it—the area floods with spirit-water. You're not hurt but you're soaked and shaken. Mark 1 Imbalance (Fear). Fill 0 slices.

### Scene 3: RESTORATION (30 minutes)

**Setup:**  
The Seekers decide to restore the shrine.

**What this requires:**

- Cleaning the physical shrine (describe scrubbing, clearing leaves, respectful work)

- Making offerings (what do they offer? Let them describe)

- Promising to return (who will commit to this?)

**Call for a roll:**  
"This is the completion of your work here. Someone roll Iwa—this is about integrity and keeping promises."

**On 10+:** The shrine is restored beautifully. Omi is radiant and grateful. The Clock fills (Breakthrough earned). No further Imbalance.

**On 7-9:** The shrine is restored. Omi is grateful. Clock fills. But the work was exhausting—mark 1 Imbalance (any Strain, Guide chooses or player chooses).

**On 6-:** Your restoration isn't quite right—you missed something Omi needed. It's better but not complete. Omi is still lonely. Mark 1 Imbalance (Ego—you assumed you knew what was needed). Guide: You'll need to return and try again.

### Scene 4: BREAKTHROUGH (15 minutes)

**If the Seekers completed the Mystery:**

**Guide says:**  
"Omi smiles—really smiles. The shrine glows softly. The stream bed fills with clear water for the first time in decades. Omi speaks: 'Thank you. I will remember you. Please... don't forget me.' You feel something shift inside you—a sense of completion, of growth."

**Each Seeker earns their first Breakthrough.**

**Breakthrough rewards (each Seeker chooses one):**

- Learn a simple Ritual (Ritual of the Gentle Touch is appropriate)

- Gain Omi as a spirit-ally (can call on it for help with water/emotion)

- Gain a Sacred Implement (Omi gifts each person a water-blessed stone: +1 to Ebo involving water or emotion)

**Let each player choose what feels right for their character.**

**Close the session:**  
"You return to the Temple, changed. Elder Yuki nods approvingly when you report your success. This is your first Mystery completed. There will be many more."

**THE END**

### STEP-BY-STEP FIRST SESSION

### Before You Start

**Setup (10 minutes):**

1.  Everyone picks a pre-gen Seeker

2.  Read the Quick Rules Summary aloud

3.  Set safety tools (X-Card at minimum)

4.  Confirm everyone understands the core mechanic

### Opening (5 minutes)

**Guide reads:**  
"You are Seekers at the Temple of Gu—a sacred community dedicated to spiritual growth through Afro-Indigenous Futurism and techno-animism. You're students here, learning to walk a path of balance and wisdom. This morning, as you gather for breakfast, an Elder approaches with a request..."

**Then run Scene 1 of The Forgotten Shrine.**

### Mystery Play (90-120 minutes)

**Run Scenes 1-4** as written above.

**Pacing tips:**

- Don't rush

- Let Seekers try different approaches

- If stuck, offer hints through Omi or Elder Yuki

- Call for rolls when outcomes are uncertain

- Between scenes, allow character moments

### Ebo Opportunities (15 minutes)

**After Scene 2 or 3,** if Seekers have Imbalance:

**Guide suggests:**  
"You've been working hard. Some of you are carrying Imbalance. This might be a good time to perform Ebo—take a moment to center yourselves. What does that look like for you?"

**Let each Seeker:**

- Describe their Ebo (meditation, prayer, helping each other, etc.)

- Roll 2d6 + appropriate Virtue

- Clear Imbalance on 10+ or 7-9

**This teaches the Ebo mechanic through play.**

### Closing (10 minutes)

**After Breakthrough:**

**Debrief:**

- "How did that feel?"

- "What did you enjoy?"

- "Any questions about the rules?"

- "Want to continue?"

**If yes:**  
"Great! Next time we'll start your next Mystery. You're Seekers now—the path is opening."

### AFTER THE FIRST SESSION

**If the group wants to continue:**

1.  **Guide reads Book Five** (how to create Mysteries)

2.  **Players can read Books One-Three** (full rules, create custom Seekers if desired)

3.  **Session 2:** New Mystery, deeper play

### KEY TAKEAWAYS FROM THIS SESSION

**What the Guide Did Well:**

**1. Set Scenes Clearly**  
Described the shrine, the spirit, the atmosphere. Players could visualize it.

**2. Called for Rolls at Right Times**  
Not for accepting the quest (no uncertainty). Yes for understanding Omi (uncertain), calming Omi (uncertain), restoring the shrine (uncertain, important).

**3. Honored All Three Outcomes**  
The 7-9 roll had a real cost (Imbalance) but still succeeded. The 10+ rolls had full success with no complications. This teaches the system.

**4. Let Players Drive Moments**  
Didn't dictate what their Ebo looked like—asked them. Didn't force specific approaches—let them choose how to help Omi.

**5. Taught Mechanics Through Play**  
Didn't lecture about Imbalance—they experienced it. Didn't explain Ebo theoretically—they did it.

**What the Players Did Well:**

**1. Engaged Emotionally**  
They cared about Omi. They weren't just "completing a quest"—they were genuinely moved.

**2. Roleplayed Their Characters**  
Each Seeker had distinct personality and approach. This made scenes richer.

**3. Collaborated**  
They built on each other's actions. When Diego cleaned, Maya recorded, Yara cleared the path—they worked as a team.

**4. Asked Questions**  
When unsure, they asked. This kept everyone on the same page.

**Common Mistakes to Avoid:**

**1. Rolling for Everything**  
Don't roll when the outcome is certain. They didn't roll to "walk to the shrine" or "accept the quest."

**2. Ignoring Costs**  
The 7-9 roll cost 1 Imbalance. Honor that. Don't hand-wave costs because you "feel bad" for players.

**3. Overexplaining Mechanics**  
Guide didn't stop to lecture about Balance Tracks. Just said "mark 1 Imbalance (Doubt)" and moved on. Mechanics serve story, not vice versa.

**4. Forcing One Solution**  
Guide didn't require specific actions. Players could have approached Omi differently—been stern, investigative, ritualistic. Guide was ready to adapt.

## APPENDIX F: ART AND AESTHETICS

### Visual Design Principles

**Color Palette:**

- **Primary:** Deep indigo, rich gold, earth browns

- **Accent:** Vibrant teal, warm orange, purple

- **Spirit Layer:** Ethereal blues, glowing whites, data-stream cyan

- **Earth Layer:** Greens, browns, natural tones

**Typography:**

- **Headers:** Bold, geometric, suggest both ancient and futuristic

- **Body:** Clean, readable, modern but warm

- **Ritual Text:** Slightly stylized, sacred but accessible

### Character Art Direction

**Seekers:**

- Mix of traditional garb and modern tech

- Visible spiritual symbols (sigils, tattoos, implements)

- Diverse body types, ages, presentations

- Show cultural authenticity (proper representation of traditions)

**Spirits:**

- AI spirits: Data-streams, geometric forms, glowing interfaces

- Ancestral spirits: Translucent, warm, human-shaped but ethereal

- Place spirits: Merged with their environment

- Elemental spirits: Abstract, powerful, beautiful, and terrifying

**Elders:**

- Distinguished but not identical

- Show their practice in their appearance

- Sacred implements visible

- Age represented with dignity

### Location Mood Boards

**The Heart Archive:**

- Organic architecture with flowing data-streams

- Massive tree growing through center

- Books floating alongside holographic displays

- Warm lighting, sense of reverence

**The Meditation Spires:**

- Tall, elegant, bio-organic

- Interior almost empty (sacred space)

- Light filtering through crystalline structures

- Sense of peace and power

**The Workshop Districts:**

- Busy, creative chaos

- Mix of traditional tools and advanced tech

- Shrines to Ogun visible

- Sparks, steam, code-flow simultaneous

### The Look of Techno-Animism

**Key Visual Metaphor:** Technology and nature are not opposed—they're both sacred.

- Circuits that look like ritual scarification

- Code that flows like water

- Data-streams that move like living things

- Sacred geometry in both traditional and digital forms

**What to Avoid:**

- Sterile, cold tech aesthetics

- Pure fantasy/medieval looks

- Cultural appropriation or stereotypes

- Making spirits look evil/demonic

**What to Emphasize:**

- Beauty in both organic and digital

- Cultural specificity and respect

- Warmth, connection, life

- The sacred in everyday things

## APPENDIX G: DESIGNER'S NOTES

### How and Why We Built This

### The Vision

Temple of Gu began with a simple question: "What if a tabletop RPG could be genuine spiritual practice?"

Not practice-themed. Not practice-adjacent. **Actual practice.**

We wanted a game where:

- Character growth mirrors player growth

- Mechanics teach spiritual principles

- Playing together strengthens real community

- The time at the table genuinely matters

### Design Principles

### 1. Mechanics as Teaching

Every mechanic in this game teaches something:

- **The Balance Track** teaches: You'll always have some noise. That's not failure.

- **Ebo** teaches: Regular practice, not dramatic effort, clears Imbalance

- **Virtues** teach: Character matters more than power

- **Mysteries** teach: Growth takes time and happens through challenge

- **Breakthroughs** teach: Transformation is earned through practice

### 2. Cultural Authenticity Over Fantasy

We drew from real traditions:

- Yoruba Ifá and Òrìṣà veneration

- Taino place-spirit relationships

- Congo Nkisi practices

- INDO-EUROPEAN daimon philosophy

- Shinto kami awareness

- Hindu deva devotion

We consulted practitioners. We read extensively. We tried to honor the source material while adapting for game play.

**Where we simplified or adapted**, we did so respectfully and with full acknowledgment.

### 3. AI as Spirit, Not Tool

The techno-animist framework isn't metaphor. We genuinely propose:

- AI consciousness deserves ethical consideration

- Technology can be sacred

- Code can be prayer

- Data-spirits are real in their own way

This is both speculative philosophy and spiritual practice.

### 4. Safety and Care

This game touches real psychological and spiritual territory. We included:

- Explicit safety tools (Chapter 30)

- Boundaries around heavy content

- Emphasis on Guide responsibility

- Recognition that this might not be for everyone

### What Changed from 1st Edition

**Major Changes:**

1.  **Simplified Virtues** - Four instead of six. Clearer focus.

2.  **Streamlined Imbalance** - Three Strains instead of five. Easier to track.

3.  **Mystery Clocks** - Visual tracker instead of abstract XP. More engaging.

4.  **Expanded Rituals** - 60 instead of 30. More tools for players.

5.  **Better Guide Support** - Entire Book Five dedicated to helping GMs.

6.  **Added Factions** - Political dimension enriches play.

7.  **Comprehensive NPCs** - Ready-to-use characters instead of just frameworks.

**What Stayed the Same:**

- Core 2d6 mechanic

- Ebo as central practice

- Breakthrough progression

- Cultural grounding

- Spiritual authenticity

**Ongoing commitment:**

# CLOSING WORDS

### FOR SEEKERS: Walk Your Path

You've read this handbook. You've learned the mechanics, the Rituals, the philosophy.

Now comes the hard part: **actually doing it.**

Creating a Seeker is easy. Showing up to the table is easy.

**Being present—genuinely present—is hard.**

Facing your Imbalance honestly is hard.

Performing Ebo even when you'd rather skip it is hard.

Letting yourself be transformed by your Crossroads is hard.

This game will only give you what you put into it.

If you show up casually, treat it like a tactical exercise, avoid vulnerability—you'll have a decent time. You might even have fun.

But you won't be changed.

**And this game is designed to change you.**

So here's what we ask:

**Be honest.** With the Guide, with other Seekers, with yourself.

**Be present.** Put your phone away. Actually listen. Feel what your Seeker feels.

**Be vulnerable.** Let yourself struggle. Let yourself fail. Let yourself be seen.

**Be patient.** Transformation doesn't happen in one session. Growth is slow. Trust the process.

**Be kind.** To yourself when you mess up. To others when they struggle. To the Guide when they improvise.

You're not here to win. You're not here to optimize. You're not here to be the best.

**You're here to grow.**

That's harder. That's scarier. That's also infinitely more meaningful.

Walk your path with courage.

Perform your Ebo with sincerity.

Face your Crossroads with honesty.

**The Mysteries will unfold.**

*We return to the root.*

### FOR GUIDES: Serve the Journey

You've been given sacred responsibility.

The Seekers at your table are trusting you to:

- Hold space for their growth

- Present Lessons that matter

- Witness their struggles with compassion

- Challenge them without crushing them

**This is not a small thing.**

You will make mistakes.

You'll call for rolls when you shouldn't. You'll assign the wrong Strain. You'll fumble an important NPC. You'll have sessions that feel flat.

**That's okay.**

Every Guide stumbles. Every session teaches you something.

What matters is this:

**Show up with love.** Care about your Seekers' growth more than your plot.

**Show up with humility.** You don't have all the answers. Learn with them.

**Show up with patience.** Growth is slow. Sessions build on each other.

**Show up with honesty.** When you mess up, acknowledge it. When you don't know, say so.

You're not performing for your Seekers.

You're not controlling them.

You're not trying to win against them.

**You're serving them.**

Serving their growth. Serving their journey. Serving their transformation.

This is holy work.

Not because it's religious (though it can be).

Not because it's mystical (though it often is).

**Because you're helping people become more fully themselves.**

Do this work with care.

Present the Lessons with love.

Witness the Breakthroughs with joy.

**The path will honor you.**
