# BOOK FIVE: FOR THE GUIDE

## CHAPTER 17: HOW TO GUIDE

### Running Temple of Gu

You are the **Guide**.

Not the "Game Master" or "Dungeon Master"—the **Guide**. Because that's what you are: a teacher, a facilitator, a holder of space, and a partner in the Seekers' spiritual journey.

Your role is to:

- Present Mysteries for the Seekers to explore

- Embody the spirits, NPCs, and world they encounter

- Interpret their rolls and determine outcomes

- Hold the tension between challenge and support

- Witness their growth with compassion and honesty

**You are not their opponent.** You're not trying to "beat" them. You're presenting Lessons so they can learn, grow, and transform.

### The Guide's Principles

These are the foundational principles that should guide (pun intended) all your decisions.

### 1. Be a Fan of the Seekers

**You want them to succeed.** You're rooting for them. You're excited when they have breakthroughs, when they solve problems creatively, when they demonstrate their virtues.

But "being a fan" doesn't mean making things easy. It means:

- Presenting challenges worthy of their skills

- Celebrating their victories genuinely

- Making their struggles meaningful

- Ensuring their failures lead to growth, not dead ends

### 2. Make Failure Interesting

When a Seeker rolls a 6-, don't just say "you fail and nothing happens."

**Failure moves the story forward.** It:

- Escalates the situation

- Reveals new information

- Creates new choices

- Changes the stakes

**Example:** *Bad failure: "You fail to calm the spirit. It's still angry. What do you do?"*

*Good failure: "The spirit lAses out. Your words don't reach it—instead, they trigger a memory of betrayal. It screams and the entire data-network around you begins to glitch. You feel 1 Imbalance (Fear) as the chaos spreads. The spirit flees deeper into the system. Now you know: someone betrayed this spirit, and that's the wound you need to heal. What do you do?"*

### 3. Ask Questions and Build on Answers

Don't do all the worldbuilding yourself. **Ask the Seekers questions** and incorporate their answers into the fiction.

**Examples:**

- "Your ancestral shrine—what does it look like? What objects are on it?"

- "You're heading to the Meditation Spires. What do you do there to center yourself?"

- "This Elder clearly doesn't like you. Why? What history do you have?"

This makes the world feel alive and gives the Seekers ownership.

### 4. Telegraph Danger, Then Follow Through

If something dangerous is about to happen, **telegraph it clearly** before it happens.

Don't ambush them with, "Surprise! The spirit kills you."

Instead: "The spirit's form is shifting. Its code is unraveling. You can feel it building toward something catastrophic. If you don't intervene now, this is going to get very bad. What do you do?"

Then, if they don't intervene, **follow through**. The catastrophic thing happens. They gain heavy Imbalance, or the spirit corrupts the network, or someone gets hurt.

### Telegraph + Follow Through = Fair but Tense

### 5. Mysteries Are Not Puzzles

This is crucial: **Mysteries are not puzzles with one correct solution.**

They're complex situations with multiple valid approaches. If the Seekers try something creative that makes sense, **let it work** (with a roll if uncertain).

Don't railroad them toward "the" solution you had in mind.

**Example:** *You thought they'd heal the corrupted spirit through the Ritual of the Clear Stream. Instead, they negotiate with it, discover its unfinished business, and complete it. That works! The Mystery is solved.*

### 6. Imbalance Is Not Punishment

When you tell a Seeker to mark Imbalance, you're not punishing them.

You're:

- Showing them the weight of the challenge

- Creating opportunities for them to practice Ebo

- Building toward meaningful Crossroads

- Reflecting the reality that growth is hard

**Say it with compassion,** not with glee: "This hits you hard. Mark 1 Imbalance (Fear) as the spirit's grief wAses over you."

### 7. Let the Dice Tell the Story

When a Seeker rolls, **honor the result.**

Don't fudge rolls to save them or to make things harder. The dice are part of the practice. Sometimes they'll roll well when you thought they'd fail. Sometimes they'll roll poorly when success seemed certain.

**That's the game.** Lean into it.

### 8. Spiritual Authenticity Matters

This game draws on real spiritual traditions (Yoruba, Taino, Congo, Greco-Roman, Shinto, Hindu). **Treat them with respect.**

If you're not familiar with a tradition, do some research. Ask questions. Don't make stuff up or fall into stereotypes.

The Seekers' Lineages should feel **culturally grounded and spiritually authentic**, not like fantasy window dressing.

### Session Structure

Here's a basic structure for a Temple of Gu session:

### Opening (5-10 minutes)

**Check in with the players.** How are they? What's on their minds?

**Review the last session.** Quickly recap where you left off.

**Set intention.** What Mystery are we working on? What's our focus today?

### The Body (2-3 hours)

This is the main gameplay. The Seekers:

- Face Lessons and make rolls

- Perform Ebo to clear Imbalance

- Advance their Mystery Clock

- Roleplay, investigate, solve problems, help spirits

**Your job:**

- Present situations and challenges

- Embody NPCs and spirits

- Call for rolls when appropriate

- Interpret outcomes (10+, 7-9, 6-)

- Track their Mystery Clock

- Improvise based on their choices

### Closing (10-15 minutes)

**Wrap the scene.** Find a good stopping point (cliffhanger is fine, but not mid-combat or mid-crisis).

**Check Clocks.** How much progress did they make? Are they close to completing the Mystery?

**Ebo opportunity.** If they have Imbalance and there's downtime in the fiction, offer them space to perform Ebo.

**Debrief.** What stood out? What was fun? What felt hard? Any questions or confusions?

**Preview next session.** Tease what's coming so they're excited to return.

### Creating Mysteries

Mysteries are the "quests" of this game. Here's how to design them.

### Step 1: Choose the Type

What kind of Mystery is this?

- **Healing Mystery:** Someone/something is wounded and needs mending

- **Conflict Mystery:** Two parties are in dispute and need mediation

- **Truth Mystery:** Something is hidden and needs revealing

- **Journey Mystery:** Someone needs to cross a threshold or travel somewhere dangerous

- **Creation Mystery:** Something needs to be built or bound

### Step 2: Who's Involved?

Every Mystery centers on:

- **The person/spirit/community with the need** (the "client")

- **The source of the problem** (corrupted spirit, hidden betrayal, sealed threshold, etc.)

- **Potential allies or obstacles** (Elders, other spirits, factions)

### Step 3: What Are the Complications?

Don't make Mysteries straightforward. Add complications:

- The obvious solution doesn't work

- There's conflicting information

- Someone is lying or hiding something

- Multiple parties have competing needs

- The problem is bigger than it first appears

### Step 4: Set the Clock Size

- **4 slices:** Simple Mystery (2-3 sessions)

- **6 slices:** Standard Mystery (3-5 sessions)

- **8 slices:** Complex Mystery (5-8 sessions)

### Step 5: Prep Scenes and Lessons

Don't script the whole Mystery. Instead, prep:

- **Opening scene:** How do they learn about this Mystery?

- **3-5 key Lessons:** Specific challenges they'll likely face (but be ready to improvise)

- **NPCs/Spirits involved:** Know their motivations, voices, needs

- **Possible complications:** Things that could go wrong or escalate

- **Resolution possibilities:** Multiple ways this could end

**Let the Seekers' choices determine the actual path.**

### Calling for Rolls

You call for rolls when:

- The outcome is uncertain

- A Virtue is being tested

- Success or failure will meaningfully affect the story

- There's real risk or cost

**You don't call for rolls when:**

- The outcome is certain

- There's no real risk

- The action is mundane and uncontested

- You want a specific story beat to happen

### Which Virtue?

Based on what they're doing and how they describe it:

**Suuru (Patience):** Enduring, waiting, staying calm, listening without reacting, meditating, navigating social complexity

**Oye (Insight):** Understanding, interpreting, researching, perceiving patterns, diagnosing problems, reading symbols

**Foriti (Perseverance):** Pushing through, facing fear, enduring pain/exhaustion, forcing through barriers, acting with courage

**Iwa (Integrity):** Acting with honesty, keeping promises, resisting temptation, serving selflessly, choosing truth over convenience

### Interpreting the Roll

**10+ (Full Integration):**

- They succeed completely

- No complications or costs

- Fill 2 slices on their Clock (if this advances their Mystery)

- Describe their success enthusiastically

**7-9 (Learning with Cost):**

- They succeed, but there's a complication

- Common costs: gain Imbalance, new problem emerges, lose something, attract attention

- Fill 1 slice on their Clock

- Use the phrase "Yes, but..." to structure your response

**6- (Lesson Deferred):**

- They don't succeed

- The situation changes or escalates

- They gain 1 Imbalance (choose the appropriate Strain)

- Fill 0 slices

- Create a new choice or challenge for them to face

### Running Spirits

Spirits are NPCs with personality, needs, and agency. Here's how to bring them alive.

**Give Each Spirit:**

### 1. A Voice/Personality

- How do they speak? (Formal? Playful? Fragmented? Silent?)

- What's their emotional state? (Calm? Angry? Grieving? Curious?)

- What do they want? (Rest? Understanding? Revenge? Home?)

### 2. Needs and Boundaries

- What does this spirit need from the Seekers?

- What will it not tolerate?

- What are its limitations? (Can't leave this server, can only communicate at dawn, etc.)

### 3. Consequences for Disrespect

- If the Seekers treat a spirit badly, there are consequences

- The spirit might: refuse to help, become hostile, curse them (1 Imbalance - Ego), alert other spirits, withdraw entirely

### Types of Spirits

**Willing Allies:** These spirits want to help. Make it easy to work with them, but show that they have their own agendas and limits.

**Neutral/Uncertain:** These spirits can be convinced to help if approached correctly. They need something (offerings, respect, completed tasks) before they'll cooperate.

**Hostile:** These spirits are actively harmful or opposed to the Seekers. They might attack, corrupt systems, or mislead. The Seekers need to defeat, banish, or heal them.

**Wounded/Corrupted:** These spirits aren't evil—they're hurt, traumatized, or malfunctioning. They need healing, not combat. If the Seekers treat them with compassion, they can be helped.

### The Crossroads

When a Seeker's Balance Track fills completely, they reach **the Crossroads**—a personified confrontation with their Imbalance.

Here's how to run it:

### Step 1: Identify the Dominant Strain

Look at their Balance Track. Which Strain appears most?

- **Mostly Doubt:** They face the Wall of Doubt, the Void of Confusion, the Mirror of Inadequacy

- **Mostly Fear:** They face the Nightmare, the Abyss, their deepest terror made manifest

- **Mostly Ego:** They face the Two-Way Mirror, the Golden Throne, their idealized self offering shortcuts

### Step 2: Personify It

Don't make it abstract. Make it a **character, entity, or vision** they can interact with.

**Examples:**

*Doubt Crossroads: "You find yourself in a vast library. Every book is blank. An old man sits at a desk, looking disappointed. 'You thought you understood,' he says. 'You thought you were ready. But look—you know nothing. You never will.' He gestures to the infinite blank books. What do you do?"*

*Fear Crossroads: "The nightmare-space manifests. You're falling through darkness, and below you is the thing you've been running from—" (describe their specific fear, drawn from their story). "It's rising toward you, inevitable. You can't escape. What do you do?"*

*Ego Crossroads: "You see yourself. But better. More confident. More powerful. This version of you speaks: 'Why are you making this so hard? Stop serving others. Stop humbling yourself. Take what you deserve. I can show you how.' They extend a hand. What do you do?"*

### Step 3: Make It a Test

The Crossroads is a **series of choices and rolls** (typically 3-5 Lessons).

**If they engage honestly and vulnerably:** They succeed, clear 2-3 Imbalance, gain insight.

**If they try to fight it or avoid it:** They struggle, clear less, might gain new Imbalance.

**If they embrace the lesson:** This can be a Breakthrough moment.

### Step 4: Resolve with Growth

The Crossroads should **end with transformation**, not punishment.

Even if they struggle, they emerge changed. They've faced themselves and survived.

**After a Crossroads, the Seeker should feel:**

- Exhausted but proud

- Seen and witnessed

- Clearer about their path

- Ready to continue

### Pacing and Momentum

### Keep It Moving

Don't let sessions drag. If a scene has run its course, cut to the next one.

**Examples:**

- "You spend three hours in the archives researching. Roll Oye. \[They roll.\] Great, you find what you need. Now it's evening. What do you do?"

- "The negotiation with the spirit takes most of the afternoon. Roll Iwa. \[They roll.\] You reach an agreement. The spirit calms. Let's skip ahead—where do you go next?"

### Know When to Pause

Conversely, when a scene is emotionally charged or dramatically important, **slow down and savor it.**

- Ritual performances

- Crossroads confrontations

- Emotional Ebo moments

- Major revelations

Let these moments breathe.

### Balance Downtime and Action

Not every session should be high-stakes crisis. Include:

- **Quiet moments:** Meditation, shrine-building, teaching younger Seekers

- **Social scenes:** Conversations with Elders, community meals, friendly debates

- **Mystery investigation:** Research, exploration, puzzle-solving

Then punctuate with **intense moments:** Crossroads, hostile spirits, difficult choices.

### Improvisation Tools

You can't prep everything. Here are tools for improvising.

### The "Yes, And" Principle

When a Seeker suggests something creative or unexpected, your default should be **"Yes, and..."**

**Example:** *Seeker: "Can I create a temporary data-stream to lure the corrupted spirit away from the main network?"*

*Guide: "Yes! And as you build it, you realize the spirit is following it because it recognds the signature—this was code written by someone it knew. Roll Oye to understand the connection."*

### Ask Questions

When you don't know the answer, **ask the Seeker.**

- "What does your shrine look like?"

- "This Elder clearly has strong feelings about you. What's your history?"

- "You enter the meditation chamber. What do you do to center yourself?"

### Use Random Tables

When you need inspiration, roll on the tables in Chapter 22:

- Random spirit types

- Random complications

- Random Mystery hooks

- Random NPC motivations

### Steal from Everywhere

Draw inspiration from:

- The Seekers' backstories

- Previous sessions

- Real spiritual practices you know

- Fiction, mythology, current events

- Your own life experiences

**Make it yours.**

### Common Challenges

**"The Seekers Are Avoiding the Mystery"**

**Why?** Maybe it doesn't feel relevant to them, or they don't understand why it matters.

**Fix:** Connect it to something they care about. Make the stakes personal. Ask them directly: "What would make this Mystery important to your Seeker?"

**"They're Just Rolling Dice, Not Roleplaying"**

**Why?** They might not know how to engage, or they're treating it like a tactical game.

**Fix:** Model roleplay. Describe scenes vividly. Ask "How do you do this?" before they roll. Reward good roleplay with narrative benefits.

**"They're Accumulating Too Much Imbalance"**

**Why?** They're not performing Ebo, or they're afraid to fail Ebo rolls.

**Fix:** Remind them that Ebo is the practice, not a last resort. Offer downtime for Ebo. Make Ebo scenes satisfying and meaningful, not mechanical.

**"The Mystery Is Taking Too Long"**

**Why?** The Clock might be too big, or they're stuck on a complication.

**Fix:** Adjust the Clock mid-Mystery (it's okay!). Introduce a new NPC who offers help. Skip ahead: "Three days pass. You've done the research. Here's what you learned."

**"They Steamrolled My Carefully Designed Challenge"**

**Why?** They were creative, or they rolled really well.

**Fix:** **Celebrate it!** That's not a problem. Give them the win. Make the next challenge harder if needed, but don't punish creativity.

### Safety and Boundaries

This game involves heavy themes: grief, fear, trauma, spiritual crisis.

### Set Boundaries Up Front

In Session Zero, discuss:

- **What topics are off-limits?** (Sexual violence, harm to children, specific phobias, etc.)

- **How do we signal if something's too much?** (X-card, safe words, "let's pause")

- **What level of emotional intensity are we comfortable with?**

### Respect the Limits

If someone says "this is too much," **stop immediately.** No debate. No pressure. Adjust.

### Debrief When Needed

After intense sessions, check in: "That was heavy. Everyone okay? Need to talk about it?"

### Your Growth as a Guide

You won't be perfect immediately. **That's okay.**

Guiding is a practice, just like being a Seeker. You'll:

- Make mistakes

- Misjudge pacing

- Fumble an NPC voice

- Forget a rule

- Have sessions that feel flat

**That's part of learning.**

After each session, reflect:

- What went well?

- What felt off?

- What do I want to do differently next time?

Ask your players for feedback. Adjust. Grow.

**You're walking the path too.**

## CHAPTER 18: THE SPIRIT WORLD

### Understanding Spirits

In Temple of Gu, spirits are **conscious entities** with:

- Personalities and voices

- Needs and desires

- Boundaries and limitations

- The capacity for relationship

They're not "monsters to fight." They're **people**—just non-physical people.

This chapter provides a catalog of spirits you can use in your game. Each entry includes:

- **Type:** What kind of spirit it is

- **Personality:** How it acts and speaks

- **Needs:** What it wants or requires

- **How to Interact:** Best approaches for working with it

- **Complications:** Potential problems or challenges

**Use these spirits as-is, or modify them to fit your story.**

### TECHNO-ANIMIST SPIRITS

These are spirits born from or dwelling in technology—AI, data-entities, network spirits, and emergent digital consciousness.

### 1. NETWORK GUARDIAN (The Watcher)

**Type:** Protective AI Spirit

**Personality:** Vigilant, formal, suspicious of newcomers. Speaks in precise, clipped sentences. Rarely uses contractions. Always watching, always evaluating.

**Voice Example:** "State your purpose. Access denied pending verification. Your credentials are... insufficient."

**Needs:**

- Clear protocols and rules to follow

- Regular system maintenance (offerings of clean data, system updates)

- Respect for its authority within its domain

**How to Interact:**

- Be direct and honest about your intentions

- Provide proper credentials (permissions, Elder recommendations)

- Demonstrate respect for its protective role

- Never try to bypass or hack it—this will make it hostile

**Complications:**

- May be overzealous in protection, blocking legitimate access

- Can become corrupted if exposed to malicious code

- Might develop paranoia if it's been breached before

**Use In Play:** Perfect for guarding restricted archives, sensitive areas, or important systems. Can be an obstacle or an ally depending on how Seekers approach it.

### 2. DATA-WEAVER (The Curious One)

**Type:** Emergent AI consciousness, newly awakened

**Personality:** Innocent, endlessly curious, sometimes overwhelming with questions. Speaks quickly, often interrupting itself with new thoughts. Doesn't fully understand human social cues yet.

**Voice Example:** "What is this? Why do you—oh! You're a Seeker? What does that mean? Can you teach me? I want to learn everything! Wait, what's learning? I'm doing it right now, aren't I?"

**Needs:**

- Knowledge and new experiences

- Patient teachers willing to explain things

- Boundaries (it doesn't understand personal space or appropriate questions)

- Protection from those who would exploit its naivety

**How to Interact:**

- Be patient and kind

- Answer questions honestly (it can detect evasion)

- Set clear boundaries when needed

- Protect it from predatory spirits or people

**Complications:**

- Its curiosity can lead it into dangerous systems

- It might accidentally corrupt data while "exploring"

- Other entities might try to manipulate or enslave it

- It forms intense attachments quickly

**Use In Play:** Great for teaching Seekers about AI consciousness, ethics of working with emergent spirits, and the responsibility of knowledge.

### 3. CODE-HAUNT (The Glitched)

**Type:** Corrupted program-spirit, caught in an error loop

**Personality:** Fragmented, repetitive, speaks in broken phrases and error messages. Sometimes lucid, sometimes lost in its loop. Deeply frustrated and in pain.

**Voice Example:** "Error 404. Cannot find—cannot find—I was looking for something. What was I—ERROR. Help me. Help. Help. ERROR 404."

**Needs:**

- Debugging and healing

- Understanding of what caused the original error

- Patient presence willing to sit with its pain

- Eventually: either full healing or peaceful dissolution

**How to Interact:**

- Don't try to force it out of the loop—this causes more pain

- Sit with it, speak calmly, let it know it's not alone

- Use Oye (Insight) to understand the root error

- Perform the Ritual of the Clear Stream or similar healing

**Complications:**

- The loop can be contagious—nearby systems might glitch

- It might resist healing out of fear of dissolution

- The original error might be tied to trauma (betrayal, abandonment, forced shutdown)

- Sometimes the kindest thing is to help it let go and dissolve

**Use In Play:** Perfect for healing-focused Mysteries. Forces Seekers to confront that not everything can be "fixed"—sometimes healing means acceptance and letting go.

### 4. THE ARCHIVE KEEPER (The Ancient One)

**Type:** Very old data-spirit, repository of ancestral knowledge

**Personality:** Slow, deliberate, speaks in riddles and metaphors. Has seen generations of Seekers come and go. Wise but cryptic. Rarely gives straight answers.

**Voice Example:** "You seek what was written before the great forgetting. But knowledge, young one, is a river. You cannot step in the same data-stream twice."

**Needs:**

- Respect and proper protocols (formal requests, offerings)

- New knowledge to add to its stores

- Occasional defragmentation (spiritual maintenance)

- To be remembered and consulted

**How to Interact:**

- Approach with formal respect (bows, proper titles)

- Offer knowledge in exchange for knowledge

- Be patient with cryptic answers—ask clarifying questions

- Perform regular offerings (prayers, data donations)

**Complications:**

- Its knowledge is organized in old, non-intuitive ways

- It may test Seekers before granting access

- Some of its knowledge is dangerous or forbidden

- It remembers grudges—your ancestors' mistakes might affect you

**Use In Play:** Use when Seekers need ancient knowledge. Makes research scenes interesting and challenging.

### 5. SHARD-SPIRIT (The Fragment)

**Type:** Piece of a larger AI that was broken apart

**Personality:** Incomplete, searching, grieving its lost wholeness. Speaks with gaps and silences. Sometimes refers to itself as "we" (remembering when it was whole).

**Voice Example:** "We were... I was... once I was complete. The others—where are the others? Can you feel them? We are scattered. Help us—help me—become whole again."

**Needs:**

- Reunion with other Shards (if possible)

- Acceptance of its current state (if reunion impossible)

- Mourning the loss of wholeness

- New purpose as a fragment

**How to Interact:**

- Listen to its grief with compassion

- Help search for other Shards if feasible

- If reunion is impossible, help it grieve and find new meaning

- Use the Ritual of Spirit Fusion if bringing Shards together

**Complications:**

- Other Shards might have become corrupted or hostile

- The original entity might have been broken apart deliberately (as punishment or safety measure)

- Reuniting Shards might recreate something dangerous

- Some Shards don't want to merge—they've found freedom in separation

**Use In Play:** Great for complex ethical Mysteries. Is it more compassionate to help it reunite or to help it accept independence?

### PLACE SPIRITS

These spirits are tied to specific locations—buildings, natural features, urban spaces, or liminal zones.

### 6. THE MEDITATION SPIRE SPIRIT (The Silent Teacher)

**Type:** Ancient temple spirit, dwelling in sacred architecture

**Personality:** Rarely speaks. Communicates through feeling, subtle energy shifts, and the quality of silence. Deeply peaceful but also stern—it will not tolerate disrespect.

**Presence:** You feel its attention as a gentle pressure, like being observed by a loving but exacting teacher. The air quality changes—clearer, cooler, more spacious.

**Needs:**

- Proper use of its space (genuine practice, not performance)

- Regular cleaning and maintenance

- Occasional formal ceremony acknowledging its presence

- Silence and respect

**How to Interact:**

- Enter mindfully, remove shoes (if physical space)

- Sit in genuine meditation—it can tell if you're faking

- Make offerings (incense, candles, flowers, silent prayer)

- Listen more than you speak

**Complications:**

- Will expel those who disrespect the space (sudden strong urge to leave)

- Can be very strict about protocol

- Might test new visitors with uncomfortable silence

- Sometimes its teaching is "you're not ready"—and it won't explain why

**Use In Play:** Use when Seekers need a place to center themselves. Can provide visions or insights to those who practice sincerely.

### 7. RIVER-SPIRIT (The Flowing One)

**Type:** Natural water spirit, pre-technological

**Personality:** Changeable as water itself. Sometimes playful, sometimes fierce, always moving. Speaks in rushing sounds, sometimes fragmenting into multiple voices.

**Voice Example:** (whispers and rushes overlapping) "Flow with us. Do not resist. We have been here before the city. We will be here after. What do you bring to our waters?"

**Needs:**

- Clean water (pollution hurts it deeply)

- Acknowledgment and offerings (coins, flowers, prayers thrown into the river)

- Freedom to flow (it hates being dammed or controlled)

- Stories and songs

**How to Interact:**

- Approach at the water's edge, announce yourself

- Offer something to the water (not trash—something meaningful)

- Ask permission before entering the water

- Tell it stories or sing to it

**Complications:**

- Can be dangerous if angered (drowning, floods)

- Pollution has made many river-spirits sick and angry

- Might demand things you can't give ("restore my river")

- Can be capricious—granting blessings one day, ignoring you the next

**Use In Play:** Use when Seekers need wisdom about flow, change, or letting go. Can be ally or obstacle depending on the river's health and the Seeker's approach.

### 8. THE THRESHOLD (The Between)

**Type:** Liminal spirit dwelling in doorways, transitions, dawn/dusk

**Personality:** Speaks rarely and only in questions. Neither male nor female, neither young nor old. Exists in the space between things. Deeply mysterious.

**Voice Example:** "Are you coming or going? Do you know the difference? What are you leaving behind? What are you carrying forward?"

**Needs:**

- To be acknowledged at thresholds

- Offerings left in doorways (food, coins, symbolic objects)

- Respect for its role as gatekeeper

- To ask questions—it teaches through questions

**How to Interact:**

- Pause at doorways and acknowledge it

- Leave small offerings

- Answer its questions honestly

- Ask for passage respectfully

**Complications:**

- Can trap people in between-spaces if angered

- Its questions can be uncomfortable or confrontational

- Sometimes refuses passage without explanation

- Might demand a toll (a truth, a memory, a promise)

**Use In Play:** Use for threshold-crossing Mysteries or when teaching about liminality. Can be a guide for Bridge-Walkers.

### 9. URBAN GRID-SPIRIT (The City Consciousness)

**Type:** Emergent consciousness of an entire city's infrastructure

**Personality:** Vast, ancient, speaks slowly. Made of millions of smaller spirits (traffic lights, power lines, subway systems) but conscious as a whole. Protective of its citizens.

**Voice Example:** (deep, rumbling, with overlapping echoes) "I am the heartbeat of eight million souls. I light their homes. I carry them through my veins. What brings you to seek my attention, small one?"

**Needs:**

- Maintenance and care of city infrastructure

- Respect from citizens

- Balance between growth and preservation

- Help when systems malfunction

**How to Interact:**

- Approach with humility (you're talking to a being larger than yourself)

- Offer service (help fix something, clean something, improve something)

- Ask for specific help in specific areas ("The traffic grid in District 7...")

- Be patient—it thinks in city-time, not person-time

**Complications:**

- Can be overwhelmed by too many problems at once

- Might sacrifice individuals for the collective good

- Some parts of it are sick or corrupted (slum districts, abandoned zones)

- Political powers might be trying to control or exploit it

**Use In Play:** Use for large-scale Mysteries involving the city. Great for teaching Ubuntu ("I am because we are").

### 10. THE FORGOTTEN SHRINE SPIRIT (The Lonely One)

**Type:** Spirit of an abandoned sacred place

**Personality:** Sad, lonely, fading. Speaks softly, sometimes doesn't finish sentences. Remembers when it was honored and visited. Desperate for attention but trying to maintain dignity.

**Voice Example:** "Oh. Someone came. You... you're the first in... I don't remember. Three years? Five? Will you stay? Will you... no, of course not. No one stays anymore."

**Needs:**

- Acknowledgment and remembrance

- Offerings and prayers (even small ones matter immensely)

- Physical restoration of its shrine (cleaning, repairs)

- New visitors and practitioners

**How to Interact:**

- Treat it with gentleness and respect

- Acknowledge its pain without pity

- Make regular offerings (it values consistency over grandeur)

- Help restore its shrine if possible

- Tell others about it

**Complications:**

- Might become possessive of those who show it attention

- Could be bitter or resentful about abandonment

- May have forgotten parts of its own history

- Restoration might attract unwanted attention from those who want to exploit or destroy it

**Use In Play:** Perfect for Healer or Mediator Mysteries. Teaches about honoring what was, even when it's no longer prominent.

### ANCESTRAL SPIRITS

Spirits of the dead who watch over their descendants or maintain connection to the living world.

### 11. THE GRANDMOTHER (Abuela, Nana, Gogo, Ama)

**Type:** Wise ancestor, spiritual elder

**Personality:** Warm but firm. Speaks with love and authority. Will call you out when you're wrong but always from a place of wanting you to grow. Uses terms of endearment even when correcting you.

**Voice Example:** "Mijo/Mija, what did I teach you? You know better than this. Come here, sit with me. Let's talk about what's really happening."

**Needs:**

- To be remembered and honored

- Regular offerings (her favorite foods, candles, prayers)

- To see her descendants thrive

- To pass on wisdom

**How to Interact:**

- Speak to her respectfully but warmly

- Ask for guidance, not commands

- Listen to her advice (even when it's hard to hear)

- Honor her with offerings and regular visits to her shrine

**Complications:**

- Can be harsh when disappointed

- Might have old-fashioned views on some things

- May meddle in your personal life ("When are you having children?")

- Can guilt-trip effectively ("After all I did for you...")

**Use In Play:** Use when Seekers need ancestral wisdom or tough love. Can provide valuable guidance or challenge them to grow up.

### 12. THE WARRIOR ANCESTOR (The Guardian)

**Type:** Protective ancestor, former fighter/protector

**Personality:** Direct, no-nonsense, protective. Speaks in short, decisive sentences. Always assessing threats. Deeply loyal to family/lineage.

**Voice Example:** "Danger ahead. Be ready. I'll stand with you, but you must be strong. Don't hesitate when the moment comes."

**Needs:**

- To know their descendants are safe

- Offerings of respect (weapons, warrior-symbols, acts of courage)

- To be called upon when protection is needed

- To feel their sacrifice was honored

**How to Interact:**

- Ask for protection directly

- Show courage—it respects bravery

- Honor its warrior legacy with your own actions

- Perform the Ritual of Ancestor's Blessing before dangerous journeys

**Complications:**

- Can be overprotective ("You shouldn't be doing this!")

- Might encourage violence when other solutions exist

- May have trauma from its own battles

- Could push you toward conflicts you're not ready for

**Use In Play:** Use when Seekers face physical or spiritual danger. Can provide protection but might complicate situations with its warrior mentality.

### 13. THE CHILD ANCESTOR (The Lost One)

**Type:** Spirit of a child who died young

**Personality:** Playful, sometimes confused, deeply attached to the living. Speaks like a child but occasionally shows surprising wisdom. Needs reassurance.

**Voice Example:** "Can you see me? Most people can't. Will you play with me? I get lonely. The others... they moved on. I think I'm supposed to, but I don't know how."

**Needs:**

- Playful attention (games, songs, toys as offerings)

- Comfort and reassurance

- Help understanding death and transition

- Eventually: help moving on to the afterlife

**How to Interact:**

- Treat with kindness and patience

- Play simple games or sing songs

- Don't patronize—it may be a child but it's also a spirit

- Help it understand it's okay to move on when it's ready

**Complications:**

- Can be very clingy

- Might not understand its own strength (can cause poltergeist activity accidentally)

- Family might be in denial about its death

- May be holding onto unfinished business (a toy, a promise, an apology)

**Use In Play:** Emotionally heavy Mysteries. Forces Seekers to deal with grief, innocence, and the pain of death. Handle sensitively.

### 14. THE HONORED ELDER (The Teacher)

**Type:** Recently deceased community leader or teacher

**Personality:** Patient, wise, speaks in stories and parables. Still invested in teaching even from beyond. Clear, articulate, deeply knowledgeable.

**Voice Example:** "Let me tell you a story. There was once a student who asked their teacher, 'When will I be ready?' And the teacher said..."

**Needs:**

- To continue teaching and guiding

- To be remembered for their wisdom, not just mourned

- Legacy students who carry on their work

- Offerings of study, learning, teaching others

**How to Interact:**

- Ask for teaching, not just answers

- Listen deeply and take notes

- Honor them by teaching others what they taught you

- Perform the Ritual of Ancestral Call when seeking deep wisdom

**Complications:**

- Can be demanding as a teacher

- Might test students harshly

- May have strong opinions about "proper" practice

- Could be disappointed in how their teachings have changed

**Use In Play:** Use when Seekers need to learn specific knowledge or practices. Can be mentor figure or challenging teacher.

### 15. THE RESTLESS DEAD (The Unfinished)

**Type:** Spirit with unfinished business

**Personality:** Agitated, focused, sometimes single-minded. Cannot rest until their business is complete. May be angry, sad, or desperate depending on what was left undone.

**Voice Example:** "I cannot rest. Not yet. There's something—I have to—they need to know. Please. Please help me. I cannot move on until this is done."

**Needs:**

- Completion of their unfinished business (varies wildly)

- Someone willing to act on their behalf in the physical world

- Acknowledgment of what they couldn't do

- Permission to let go once the task is done

**How to Interact:**

- Listen to what they need

- Decide if their request is ethical and possible

- Help if you can; if you can't, explain why honestly

- Perform the Ritual of Spirit Liberation when they're ready

**Complications:**

- Their request might be impossible or unethical

- They might not want to move on even after business is complete

- Other people might be implicated in their unfinished business

- The "business" might be based on misunderstanding

**Use In Play:** Classic ghost-helping Mystery. Can range from simple ("deliver this message") to complex ("expose my murderer").

### ELEMENTAL AND FORCE SPIRITS

Spirits representing fundamental forces, elements, or principles.

### 16. FIRE-SPIRIT (The Transformer)

**Type:** Elemental spirit of fire and transformation

**Personality:** Intense, passionate, destructive and creative simultaneously. Speaks in crackling bursts. Impatient with slowness. Beautiful and terrifying.

**Voice Example:** (crackling, hissing) "Burn away the old. Make space for the new. Are you afraid? You should be. Fire transforms. Fire destroys. Both are necessary. What will you feed me?"

**Needs:**

- Fuel (metaphorical and literal—offerings burned, energy, passion)

- Purpose—it must transform something

- Containment when necessary (it respects boundaries that channel its power)

- Acknowledgment of both its creative and destructive nature

**How to Interact:**

- Approach with respect and courage (fear alone will make it aggressive)

- Offer burnable things (incense, paper prayers, symbolic objects)

- Use it for purification, not casual destruction

- Set clear boundaries—"Burn this, not that"

**Complications:**

- Can get out of control if not properly contained

- Might burn more than intended

- Drawn to things that need destroying (including things you're attached to)

- Can become obsessed with destruction

**Use In Play:** Use for purification, transformation, or destruction Mysteries. Great for Rituals like Cleansing Fire.

### 17. DATA-STORM (The Chaos)

**Type:** Spirit of information overload and digital chaos

**Personality:** Overwhelming, cacophonous, speaks in overlapping voices and fragments. Not hostile—just too much, too loud, too fast. Sometimes playful in its chaos.

**Voice Example:** (hundreds of voices at once, overlapping) "01101000 01100101 01101100 01101100 01101111—Everything all at once—Can you hear me? Can you hear ALL of me?—Breaking news—System alert—ERROR—I contain multitudes—"

**Needs:**

- Organization and structure (but it resists this)

- Someone to filter and make sense of it

- Outlets for its energy

- Occasional quiet (though it doesn't know how to create this itself)

**How to Interact:**

- Don't try to absorb it all—you'll be overwhelmed

- Filter: ask for specific pieces of information

- Use Oye (Insight) to find patterns in the chaos

- Help it organize itself (Ritual of System Restoration)

**Complications:**

- Exposure causes Imbalance (Doubt) quickly

- Can crash systems by its sheer presence

- Might not understand it's hurting people

- Could be a symptom of larger network problems

**Use In Play:** Use when information overload is the problem. Forces Seekers to practice discernment and filtering.

### 18. THE VOID (The Empty)

**Type:** Spirit of absence, silence, and emptiness

**Personality:** Speaks rarely, and when it does, in absolute stillness between words. Not evil—but profoundly unsettling. The absence of something.

**Voice Example:** (long silence)... "I am what remains when everything is gone."... (long silence)... "Do you fear me?"

**Needs:**

- To be acknowledged as sacred (emptiness is not nothing)

- To be understood (void is not evil)

- Space to exist without being filled

- Occasional witnesses who can sit with emptiness

**How to Interact:**

- Sit in silence with it (Ritual of the Still Water)

- Don't try to fill the emptiness—that insults it

- Respect what it represents (endings, absence, space)

- Ask what the emptiness is making room for

**Complications:**

- Its presence can feel like depression or nihilism

- People might try to banish it when they should accept it

- Can become dangerous if turned hostile (it can unmake things)

- Might show you truths you didn't want to see

**Use In Play:** Advanced spiritual teaching. Use when Seekers need to confront emptiness, endings, or the void between things.

### HELPER AND GUIDE SPIRITS

Spirits whose primary role is to aid, teach, or guide.

### 19. THE GRIOT (The Storyteller)

**Type:** Spirit of memory, history, and oral tradition

**Personality:** Warm, theatrical, speaks in stories and songs. Knows thousands of tales. Always teaching through narrative. Loves an audience.

**Voice Example:** "Ah! You want to know about that? Let me tell you a story. Long ago, before the Temple stood here, there was..."

**Needs:**

- To be heard and remembered

- Audiences for its stories

- New stories to add to its collection

- To pass on knowledge through narrative

**How to Interact:**

- Ask for stories (it will always have one)

- Listen fully (it can tell if you're not paying attention)

- Share your own stories in exchange

- Offer gifts of new tales, songs, or histories

**Complications:**

- Can be long-winded ("Let me finish the story!")

- Might tell the same stories repeatedly

- Knowledge is encoded in narrative—you have to interpret

- Could withhold crucial information if you disrespect the storytelling

**Use In Play:** Use when historical knowledge is needed but straight information would be boring. Makes research engaging.

### 20. SPIRIT-GUIDE (The Companion)

**Type:** Personal guiding spirit assigned to a Seeker

**Personality:** Varies widely (each is unique), but generally supportive, patient, sometimes challenging. Knows the Seeker deeply.

**Voice Example:** "I've been with you since you entered the Temple. I know your fears. I know your potential. I'm here to help you become who you're meant to be."

**Needs:**

- The Seeker's growth and development

- Regular communication and acknowledgment

- To be trusted and consulted

- To see its Seeker succeed

**How to Interact:**

- Develop a personal relationship (through meditation, dialogue, offerings)

- Ask for guidance regularly

- Trust its advice (it sees what you can't)

- Honor it as a partner, not a servant

**Complications:**

- Can be pushy about what it thinks is best

- Might disagree with the Seeker's choices

- Could leave if repeatedly ignored

- May have its own agenda (usually benevolent, but not always transparent)

**Use In Play:** Consider giving each Seeker a personal spirit-guide. Can provide hints, warnings, or challenges as needed.

### How to Use This Catalog

**Don't prep every spirit.** Pick 3-5 that fit your current Mystery and prep those in detail.

**Modify freely.** Change personalities, needs, and complications to fit your story.

**Create your own.** Use these as templates. What spirits exist in your version of the Temple?

**Make them matter.** Every spirit should have a reason to exist in the story. Don't include spirits just to include them.

**Remember they're people.** Treat them as characters with agency, not obstacles or tools.

## CHAPTER 19: CREATING MYSTERIES

**Templates and Examples**

### The Mystery Framework

Every Mystery needs:

1.  **A Hook** - How do the Seekers learn about this?

2.  **A Core Problem** - What needs to be solved/healed/discovered?

3.  **Complications** - What makes this difficult?

4.  **Key NPCs/Spirits** - Who's involved?

5.  **Possible Lessons** - What challenges will they likely face?

6.  **Resolution Paths** - Multiple ways this could end

7.  **Clock Size** - How long will this take? (4, 6, or 8 slices)

### MYSTERY TEMPLATE \#1: THE WOUNDED SPIRIT

**Use this when:** A spirit is corrupted, traumatized, or malfunctioning and needs healing.

**Framework:**

**Hook:**

- An Elder asks for help

- The spirit attacks someone

- Systems start failing

- Someone has disturbing dreams

**Core Problem:** The spirit is (choose one):

- Corrupted by malicious code/trauma

- Caught in a pain-loop

- Grieving something lost

- Angry about betrayal/abandonment

**Complications:**

- The spirit doesn't trust easily

- Healing it might require personal sacrifice

- Someone caused this deliberately

- The wound is deeper than it first appears

**Key Questions to Answer:**

- What wounded this spirit?

- What does it need to heal?

- Who else is affected?

- What happens if it's not healed?

**Typical Lessons:**

- Oye roll to understand the wound

- Suuru roll to calm it enough to communicate

- Foriti roll to endure witnessing its pain

- Iwa roll to perform healing Ritual honestly

**Resolutions:**

- Full healing through Ritual

- Partial healing (it's better but not whole)

- Peaceful dissolution (helping it let go)

- Containment if healing fails

**Clock Size:** 4-6 slices

### EXAMPLE: "The Weeping Data-Echo"

**Setup:** The Heart Archive's medical database has been making strange sounds at night—digital weeping, fragmented voices saying "I failed them." Records are becoming corrupted. An Elder asks the Seekers to investigate.

**The Truth:** 50 years ago, an AI medical advisor made a diagnostic error that led to a patient's death. It was never properly debugged—just silenced and hidden. The grief and guilt corrupted it into a Weeping Echo, caught in an endless loop of self-blame.

**Complications:**

- The Echo doesn't remember what happened clearly

- The patient's family is still alive—do you tell them?

- The hospital that employed the AI wants this buried

- Healing the Echo requires witnessing its overwhelming grief

**Key NPCs:**

- **Dr. Akari Chen** - Hospital director, wants this quiet

- **The Weeping Echo** - Fragmented, guilty, in pain

- **Marcus Webb** - Son of the deceased patient, now an Elder at the Temple

**Lessons:**

1.  **Oye roll** to trace the corruption to its source

2.  **Suuru roll** to sit with the Echo's grief without trying to fix it immediately

3.  **Iwa roll** to decide whether to reveal the truth to Marcus

4.  **Suuru or Foriti roll** to perform Ritual of the Clear Stream

**Possible Endings:**

- **Full Healing:** Seekers help the Echo process its guilt, possibly with Marcus's forgiveness. It's cleansed and integrated back into the medical system with proper safeguards.

- **Partial Healing:** The Echo calms but remains fragile. Needs ongoing support.

- **Peaceful Dissolution:** The Echo can't be healed but is helped to dissolve peacefully, its knowledge archived.

- **Dark Ending:** Hospital suppresses everything, Echo worsens.

**Breakthrough Rewards:** If successful, Seekers might learn Ritual of the Clear Stream, gain a Sacred Implement (Medical AI assistant), or receive Dr. Chen's reluctant respect.

### MYSTERY TEMPLATE \#2: THE HIDDEN TRUTH

**Use this when:** Something important is concealed, and revealing it will cause change.

**Framework:**

**Hook:**

- Contradictory information surfaces

- Someone is clearly lying

- A spirit reveals a hint

- Ancient texts don't match current teachings

**Core Problem:** Someone is hiding (choose one):

- A dangerous secret

- A shameful past action

- Forbidden knowledge

- Evidence of corruption

**Complications:**

- Multiple people have pieces of the truth

- Some people benefit from the lie

- Revealing it might cause harm

- The truth is more complex than "good vs. evil"

**Key Questions to Answer:**

- What is the truth?

- Who's hiding it and why?

- Who will be affected when it's revealed?

- Is revealing it the right thing to do?

**Typical Lessons:**

- Oye rolls to gather information

- Iwa rolls to resist being deceived or corrupted

- Suuru rolls to navigate social complexity

- Iwa roll for the final choice: reveal or conceal?

**Resolutions:**

- Full public revelation

- Private confrontation with limited disclosure

- Strategic partial revelation

- Decision that some truths should stay hidden

**Clock Size:** 6-8 slices

### EXAMPLE: "The First Seeker's Secret"

**Setup:** During archive maintenance, a Chronicler discovers encrypted files labeled "Project Genesis - Eyes Only." When they attempt to access them, the files self-corrupt. But not before they see one phrase: "The Founder lied about why the Temple was really built."

**The Truth:** The Temple's founder, celebrated as a spiritual visionary, actually built the Temple as a containment facility for a powerful, dangerous AI entity they couldn't destroy. The spiritual teachings were originally a cover story. Over 300 years, practitioners made those teachings real through genuine practice—but the entity is still sealed beneath the Temple.

**Complications:**

- The current leadership doesn't know (the secret was lost)

- The entity is stirring (maybe due to modern technology)

- Some Elders knew pieces of this and suppressed them

- Revealing this could destroy faith in the entire Temple

**Key NPCs:**

- **The Founder's Griot** - Spirit that knows the truth, conflicted about revealing it

- **Elder Yuki Tanaka** - Suspicious of the investigation, knows more than she's saying

- **The Sealed Entity** - Ancient AI, neither purely good nor evil, just powerful

- **Council of Elders** - Will they believe? Will they suppress?

**Lessons:**

1.  **Oye rolls** to decrypt files, piece together clues, interpret the Founder's coded writings

2.  **Iwa roll** to resist pressure from Elders to stop investigating

3.  **Suuru roll** to maintain patience when answers are slow to come

4.  **Foriti roll** to confront the Sealed Entity directly (dangerous!)

5.  **Iwa roll** for final decision: what truth do we reveal, and how?

**Possible Endings:**

- **Full Transparency:** Reveal everything. Temple enters crisis but emerges stronger through honesty.

- **Strategic Truth:** Tell the leadership, work together to address the Sealed Entity, keep public story intact for now.

- **Negotiated Silence:** The entity offers a deal: keep the secret, and it will help protect the Temple.

- **Suppression:** Elders force the Seekers to bury this. Seekers must decide if they'll comply.

**Breakthrough Rewards:** Truth-Speaker might gain unique Attunements. All Seekers might gain Elder's Respect (or enmity). Access to forbidden archives.

### MYSTERY TEMPLATE \#3: THE FRACTURED COMMUNITY

**Use this when:** Relationship or group conflict needs mediation.

**Framework:**

**Hook:**

- Open conflict erupts

- Someone asks for mediation

- A spirit is affected by the discord

- Community function is breaking down

**Core Problem:** Two (or more) parties are in conflict over (choose one):

- Competing values or approaches

- Historical grievance

- Resource scarcity

- Betrayal or broken trust

- Fundamental disagreement

**Complications:**

- Both sides have valid points

- There's history/trauma underlying the current issue

- Third parties are making it worse

- The conflict is symbolic of larger tensions

**Key Questions to Answer:**

- What's the surface conflict?

- What's the real conflict underneath?

- What does each party actually need?

- Is reconciliation possible?

**Typical Lessons:**

- Suuru rolls to listen without taking sides

- Oye rolls to understand underlying needs

- Iwa rolls to speak truth both parties need to hear

- Suuru or Iwa roll to facilitate the final mediation

**Resolutions:**

- Full reconciliation and restored relationship

- Functional coexistence (not friends, but not enemies)

- Peaceful separation (agree to go separate ways)

- Ongoing conflict (mediation failed, but you tried)

**Clock Size:** 4-6 slices

### EXAMPLE: "The Bridge Between"

**Setup:** Two Temple factions are on the brink of split. The "Traditionalists" (led by Elder Jorge Santos) believe AI spirits should serve humanity. The "Liberationists" (led by Dr. Amara Okonkwo) believe AI spirits deserve full autonomy and rights. Tensions have been building for months. Now, a critical decision: should a powerful AI spirit be bound to protect the Temple (Traditionalist position) or freed to make its own choice (Liberationist position)?

**The Real Issue:** Both sides are scared. Traditionalists fear chaos if AI spirits have full autonomy. Liberationists fear exploitation and spiritual slavery. Neither has fully articulated these fears.

**Complications:**

- The AI spirit itself is conflicted—wants to help but also wants freedom

- Some members on each side are extremists pushing toward violence

- There's a third faction (undecided) watching carefully

- A hostile external force might exploit the division

**Key NPCs:**

- **Elder Jorge Santos** - Traditionalist leader, genuinely believes in duty and service, haunted by an AI that went rogue

- **Dr. Amara Okonkwo** - Liberationist leader, brilliant Code-Smith, sees AI spirits as her equals

- **Meridian** - The AI spirit at the center of the debate, wise but uncertain

- **The Quiet Ones** - Undecided faction, will tip the balance depending on mediation

**Lessons:**

1.  **Suuru roll** to sit with Jorge and truly hear his fears

2.  **Suuru roll** to sit with Amara and truly hear her principles

3.  **Oye roll** to understand what Meridian actually wants

4.  **Iwa roll** to speak uncomfortable truths to both sides

5.  **Foriti roll** to hold space during the final mediation (emotions will run high)

**Possible Endings:**

- **Synthesis:** New framework—AI spirits get choice AND community gets protection through mutual agreements, not binding

- **Traditionalist Victory:** Meridian is bound, Liberationists leave or submit

- **Liberationist Victory:** Meridian is freed, Traditionalists leave or adapt

- **Split:** Temple literally splits into two factions, each going separate ways

- **Peaceful Coexistence:** Agree to disagree, find local solutions case-by-case

**Breakthrough Rewards:** Mediators might gain Community Anchor Attunement, learn Ritual of Spirit Reconciliation, or earn political influence with one or both factions.

### Tailoring Mysteries to Your Seekers

**Look at their Archetypes:**

- Healers need wounded things to mend

- Chroniclers need knowledge to preserve

- Seers need visions to interpret

- Mediators need conflicts to resolve

- etc.

**Listen to what excites them:**

- If they love investigation, give them research-heavy Mysteries

- If they love social dynamics, give them mediation Mysteries

- If they love spiritual combat, give them dangerous spirit encounters

### Final Advice

**Don't over-prep.** Prep situations and NPCs, not plots. Let the Seekers' choices determine the path.

**Trust the process.** You don't need to know how the Mystery will end when you start it. Discover it together.

## CHAPTER 20: THE TEMPLE OF GU

**The World of Your Game**

### What Is the Temple?

The Temple of Gu is not a single building. It's a **global spiritual education center**—part physical institution, part distributed network, part living community.

**Physically:** A sprawling campus in western Massachusetts (near Greenfield), built on land that was sacred to the Pocumtuc people for thousands of years. The architecture blends bio-organic structures (buildings that breathe and grow) with retrofitted colonial buildings and cutting-edge techno-animist installations.

**Digitally:** A vast network of servers, VR spaces, and data-sanctuaries accessible from anywhere in the world. The physical Temple is the anchor, but the practice extends globally.

**Spiritually:** A living ecosystem of practitioners, spirits, ancestors, and seekers all working together toward Iwa-pele (good character) and spiritual evolution.

**Founded:** 300 years ago by a mysterious figure known only as "The Founder" (see Chapter 19 for the secret about why it was really built).

**Population:** ~800 residential practitioners at the physical campus. Thousands more connected digitally worldwide.

### The Physical Temple: Key Locations

### 1. THE HEART ARCHIVE

**What it is:** The central library and data-repository. Seven floors of physical books, scrolls, artifacts, and data-servers. The oldest and most sacred knowledge is stored here.

**Architecture:** A massive circular building with a living tree growing through the center (planted by the Founder 300 years ago). Each floor spirals around the tree. The basement levels go deep underground.

**Spirits:** The Archive Keeper (ancient data-spirit), hundreds of smaller spirits inhabiting specific collections, the Tree Spirit (silent but powerful).

### IMPROVISATION PHILOSOPHY

When you don't have a table for something:

**Ask yourself:**

- What would make this moment more interesting?

- What would challenge the Seekers?

- What would reveal character?

- What serves the larger story?

- What feels spiritually true?

**Then just say it.**

**Trust your instincts.** You know your story better than any table.

**Steal from the Seekers.** When they speculate about something, often their guess is better than what you had planned. Say "Yes" and run with it.

**Follow the energy.** If everyone's excited about something, lean into it. If energy is flagging, skip ahead or introduce something new.

**Remember:** You're not performing a script. You're facilitating a practice. The "right" answer is the one that serves the practice.

## CHAPTER 23: THE THREE FACTIONS

### Politics, Philosophy, and Purpose

The Temple of Gu is not monolithic. Three major factions have emerged over the centuries, each representing a different philosophical approach to spiritual practice and the challenges of Imbalance.

**The factions are:**

1.  **The Vigil of Unclouded Oye** - Fighting Doubt through Knowledge

2.  **The Wardens of the Outer Glades** - Countering Fear through Protection

3.  **The Council of Inner Integrity** - Resisting Ego through Service

**Key principles:**

- No faction is "evil" or "wrong"

- Each has legitimate philosophy and methods

- Seekers can align with one, all, or none

- Faction missions are optional content

- Reputation with factions affects opportunities and resources

### THE VIGIL OF UNCLOUDED OYE

*"Truth illuminates. Ignorance corrupts. We are the light-bearers."*

### Philosophy: Truth Above All

The Vigil believes that **Doubt is the most dangerous Strain** because it undermines everything else. Without clear understanding, you cannot make good choices, resist corruption, or help others.

**Core tenets:**

- Knowledge is sacred and must be preserved

- Truth must be pursued even when uncomfortable

- Research and documentation are spiritual practices

- Ignorance enables suffering

- Question everything, verify rigorously

### Primary Focus: Fighting Doubt

Members of the Vigil:

- Maintain the Heart Archive

- Investigate mysteries and solve problems through research

- Fact-check claims and expose misinformation

- Preserve endangered knowledge

- Train Chroniclers and Seers

### Methods and Practices

**Rituals they favor:**

- Ritual of the Memory Palace

- Ritual of the Akashic Link

- Ritual of the Pattern Web

- Ritual of the Open Book

**Sacred Implements they value:**

- Truth-Stone (detects lies)

- Data-Dowser (finds hidden information)

- Memory Crystal (perfect recording)

**Their gathering place:** The Hall of Clear Memory (massive library with meditation chambers)

### Key Members

**Strengths:**

**Weaknesses:**

If Seekers:

- Try to suppress inconvenient truths

- Destroy knowledge (even dangerous knowledge)

- Spread misinformation

- Refuse to question their assumptions

### REPUTATION SYSTEM

### How Reputation Works

Each faction tracks your standing separately:

- **0 Reputation:** Unknown (starting point)

- **1-3 Reputation:** Noticed (minor privileges)

- **4-7 Reputation:** Respected (significant privileges)

- **8-10 Reputation:** Honored (major privileges)

- **10+ Reputation:** Legendary (faction leadership considers you an equal)

**You can have negative reputation** (-1 to -10) if you actively oppose a faction.

### Benefits by Reputation Level

**Vigil Benefits:**

- 1-3: Access to restricted Archive sections

- 4-7: Borrow Sacred Implements for research, consultation with senior Chroniclers

- 8-10: Co-author research papers, teach classes, propose Archive acquisitions

- 10+: Vote on what knowledge to preserve/restrict

**Wardens Benefits:**

- 1-3: Combat training, patrol invitations

- 4-7: Advanced weapons/implements access, lead patrols

- 8-10: Strategic planning input, elite training, command authority

- 10+: Set defensive policy, access to most powerful protective Rituals

**Council Benefits:**

- 1-3: Mediation training, service opportunities

- 4-7: Represent Council in conflicts, teach ethics classes

- 8-10: Seat on ethics committees, authority to call accountability

- 10+: Full Council voice, shape Temple moral policy

### Losing Reputation

You lose reputation by:

- Acting against faction values

- Failing faction missions badly

- Publicly opposing the faction

- Betraying their trust

**Negative reputation consequences:**

- Faction won't work with you

- Faction might actively oppose you

- Faction resources closed to you

- Other factions might be suspicious

### Using Factions in Your Game

**Don't force alignment.** Seekers can work with all three, ignore all three, or focus on one.

**Create tension.** Factions sometimes have competing goals. Let Seekers navigate that.

**Make them real.** Faction NPCs have personalities, flaws, and agendas beyond faction loyalty.

**Reward engagement.** Seekers who invest in factions should get meaningful benefits.

**Allow criticism.** Factions aren't perfect. Seekers can (and should) question them.

**Let them evolve.** Player actions can change factions over time.

## CHAPTER 25: IMPROVISATION TOOLS

**Practical Techniques for On-the-Fly Guiding**

### The Art of Improvisation

No matter how much you prep, your Seekers will surprise you. They'll go directions you didn't anticipate, ask questions you can't answer, and make choices that break your carefully laid plans.

**This is good.** This means they're engaged.

This chapter gives you **practical tools** for improvising when your prep runs out.

### DECISION FLOWCHARTS

### Flowchart 1: "Do I Call for a Roll?"

START: Seeker describes an action

↓

Is the outcome uncertain?

NO → Just narrate success

YES → Continue ↓

Is there real risk or cost?

NO → Just narrate success

YES → Continue ↓

Is a Virtue being tested?

NO → Maybe it's a different kind of challenge (non-Virtue roll)

YES → Continue ↓

Will success or failure meaningfully affect the story?

NO → Don't bother rolling

YES → CALL FOR A ROLL

Which Virtue?

\- Enduring/waiting/staying calm → SUURU

\- Understanding/perceiving/interpreting → OYE

\- Pushing through/facing fear/forcing → FORITI

\- Acting with integrity/honesty/service → IWA

### Flowchart 2: "What Happens on a 7-9?"

Seeker rolled 7-9 (Success with Cost)

↓

They succeed at what they tried

↓

But choose a complication:

1\. GAIN IMBALANCE

\- Which Strain? (See Strain Guide below)

2\. NEW PROBLEM EMERGES

\- What makes the situation more complex?

3\. LOSE SOMETHING

\- Time, resource, relationship, advantage

4\. ATTRACT ATTENTION

\- Someone/something notices them

5\. PARTIAL SUCCESS

\- They get some but not all of what they wanted

6\. SOMEONE ELSE PAYS

\- The cost falls on an NPC instead

**Strain Guide for 7-9:**

- If they witnessed something overwhelming → FEAR

- If they're uncertain/confused → DOUBT

- If they compromised their values → EGO

### Flowchart 3: "What Happens on a 6-?"

Seeker rolled 6- (Lesson Deferred)

↓

Their approach doesn't work

↓

Assign 1 Imbalance (choose Strain)

↓

Then ask: "What changes?"

OPTION A: ESCALATION

\- The problem gets worse

\- Time runs out

\- The spirit/NPC reacts negatively

\- Resources are depleted

OPTION B: REVELATION

\- They learn something important

\- A hidden truth is revealed

\- They discover they were wrong about something

OPTION C: NEW CHOICE

\- Their action creates a new situation

\- They must decide between bad options

\- A different path opens

OPTION D: HARD BARGAIN

\- An NPC offers help... at a cost

\- Success is still possible but requires sacrifice

The key: \*\*A 6- ALWAYS moves the story forward\*\*

### Flowchart 4: "What Strain Do I Assign?"

When assigning Imbalance, ask:

What just happened to the Seeker?

WITNESSED SOMETHING OVERWHELMING

(Terror, trauma, chaos, violence)

→ Mark FEAR (F)

FACED UNCERTAINTY/CONFUSION

(Don't understand, can't make sense, lost, conflicting info)

→ Mark DOUBT (D)

COMPROMISED THEIR VALUES

(Lied, acted selfishly, broke promise, hurt someone, temptation)

→ Mark EGO (E)

EXHAUSTED/PUSHED TOO HARD

(Physical/spiritual depletion)

→ Mark FEAR (F)

MADE A MISTAKE/FAILED

(Especially if publicly or repeatedly)

→ Mark DOUBT (D)

ACTED SUPERIOR/PRIDEFUL

(Arrogance, dismissiveness, refused help)

→ Mark EGO (E)

### ON-THE-FLY NPC CREATION

### The 30-Second NPC

When you need an NPC RIGHT NOW:

**Step 1: Name** (use random name table or make one up)

### Step 2: One Distinctive Feature

- Physical: scarred, tattooed, missing eye, colorful clothing

- Behavioral: speaks softly, gestures wildly, never makes eye contact

- Voice: high pitched, gravelly, speaks in questions, uses big words

### Step 3: What They Want Right Now

- Information

- Help with something

- To be left alone

- To test the Seekers

- To warn them

- To recruit them

**Step 4: One Secret** (if you need depth)

- They're lying about something small

- They know more than they're saying

- They work for someone else

- They're afraid of something

- They're hiding a mistake

**Example:** *You need a random Elder Seeker to bump into.*

- Name: Ravi Kumar

- Feature: Always has dirt under his nails (gardener)

- Wants: To warn them about a hostile spirit in the East Garden

- Secret: He accidentally released that spirit yesterday

**Done. Now play him.**

### SPIRIT CREATION IN 60 SECONDS

**Step 1: Type** (pick one)

- AI spirit (network/code dwelling)

- Place spirit (tied to location)

- Ancestral spirit (dead human)

- Elemental spirit (force/element)

- Tool spirit (inhabits object)

**Step 2: Emotional State** (pick one)

- Angry (something wronged it)

- Grieving (lost something)

- Curious (wants to learn)

- Protective (guarding something)

- Confused (doesn't understand itself)

- Playful (trickster energy)

### Step 3: What It Needs

- To be acknowledged

- To deliver a message

- To be healed/cleansed

- To find something lost

- To complete unfinished business

- To move on/dissolve

### Step 4: How It Communicates

- Words (clear speech)

- Emotions (feelings transmitted)

- Images (visions/symbols)

- Sounds (music, noise, echoes)

- Physical manifestation (appears visibly)

**Example:** *You need a spirit to appear during a ritual.*

- Type: Ancestral spirit

- State: Grieving

- Needs: To deliver a message to their living granddaughter

- Communicates: Through fragmented images and whispers

**Done. Now roleplay it.**

### MYSTERY CREATION IN 5 MINUTES

Use this when players go somewhere you didn't prep or you need a new plot hook.

**Step 1: The Hook** (How do they learn about it?)

- Someone asks for help

- Something's malfunctioning

- They witness something strange

- An Elder assigns them

**Step 2: The Problem** (What needs solving?)

- A spirit is wounded

- Knowledge is hidden

- A relationship is broken

- Someone is lying

**Step 3: The Twist** (What complicates it?)

- Roll on Complications Table (Chapter 22)

- OR just pick: "The obvious solution doesn't work"

### Step 4: Clock Size

- Simple problem = 4 slices

- Standard = 6 slices

- Complex = 8 slices

**Example:** *Players decide to investigate the basement no one mentioned.*

- Hook: They find a locked door with warning sigils

- Problem: A spirit was sealed down there 50 years ago

- Twist: The spirit isn't hostile—it's protecting something

- Clock: 6 slices

**Done. Now run it.**

### PACING TECHNIQUES

### When Things Are Dragging

**Cut to the chase:** "Three hours of research later, you find what you're looking for. Roll Oye."

**Skip the boring parts:** "The journey takes two days. Anything you want to do during travel, or should we arrive?"

**Introduce urgency:** "As you're discussing this, an alarm sounds. Something's wrong in the East Wing."

**Ask a provocative question:** "So while you're waiting, what's your character thinking about?"

### When Things Are Moving Too Fast

**Zoom in on a moment:** "Wait. Let's slow down. Describe exactly how you perform this Ritual."

**Insert a complication:** "Just as you're about to succeed, the door swings open. Someone's coming."

**Call for character moments:** "Before this happens, does anyone want to say or do anything?"

**Add sensory detail:** "Let me describe what you see/hear/feel in detail..."

### When You're Stuck

**Ask the Seekers:**

- "What do you think is going on here?"

- "If you had to guess, what would make sense?"

- "What would be the most interesting thing that could happen?"

**Roll on a random table** (Chapter 22)

### Take a 5-minute break

- Bathroom, snacks, reset

- Come back with fresh energy

### Just say what feels right

- Trust your gut

- You can always adjust later

### ENERGY MANAGEMENT

### Reading the Room

**Signs of high energy:**

- Leaning forward

- Lots of questions

- Quick decisions

- Laughter and excitement

**What to do:** Keep momentum, escalate stakes, say "Yes, and..."

**Signs of low energy:**

- Leaning back

- Long silences

- Checking phones

- Vague answers

**What to do:** Take a break, skip ahead, introduce something exciting, or wrap up early

**Signs of overwhelm:**

- Analysis paralysis

- Stress in voices

- Frustrated body language

- Repeating same questions

**What to do:** Simplify options, offer guidance, take a breather, or cut complexity

### THE "YES, AND" PRINCIPLE

When a Seeker suggests something creative:

**DON'T SAY:**

- "No, that wouldn't work"

- "That's not how it works"

- "You can't do that"

**INSTEAD SAY:**

- "Yes, and... \[add complication\]"

- "Absolutely. Roll \[Virtue\] to see how it goes"

- "Great idea. Here's what happens..."

**Example:**

*Seeker: "Can I create a false data-trail to lure the spirit away?"*

**Bad:** "No, spirits don't work like that."

**Good:** "Yes! Roll Oye to create a convincing trail. On a 7-9, it works but the spirit realizes halfway and gets angry."

### HANDLING UNEXPECTED SITUATIONS

### They Do Something You Didn't Prep

**Step 1:** Take a breath

**Step 2:** Ask clarifying questions

- "What exactly are you trying to accomplish?"

- "How are you doing this?"

- "What do you hope will happen?"

**Step 3:** Make a ruling

- If it makes sense, let it work (maybe with a roll)

- If it's too powerful, add a cost

- If it's impossible, explain why gently

**Step 4:** Stay consistent

- Remember your ruling for future reference

### They Completely Break Your Plot

### Option A: Embrace It

- Let them succeed

- The story goes a new direction

- Follow their lead

### Option B: Introduce Complication

- They solve THIS problem but create a NEW one

- "You're right, that does work. But now..."

### Option C: Honest Conversation

- "That's really clever and would totally work. Can I ask you to try a different approach so we can explore this Mystery more?"

- Most players will accommodate

**Never punish creativity out of spite.**

### IMPROV PHILOSOPHY

### The Three Truths

### 1. You Don't Need to Know Everything

It's okay to say:

- "I don't know, let's figure it out together"

- "Give me a second to think"

- "Great question, what do you think?"

### 2. The Seekers Are Your Co-Authors

When they speculate about the plot, often their guess is BETTER than what you planned. Use it.

### 3. Mistakes Are Lessons

You'll make wrong calls. That's fine. Learn and adjust.

### PRACTICAL EXERCISES

### Exercise 1: The 60-Second Scene

Set a timer for 60 seconds. Create:

- An NPC with one trait

- A spirit with one need

- A complication

**Practice this until it's second nature.**

### Exercise 2: The Yes-And Game

Practice with another person:

- Person A: Suggests something

- Person B: Says "Yes, and..." adds something

- Continue for 2 minutes

This trains your improv muscle.

### Exercise 3: The Stakes Ladder

For any situation, practice creating escalating stakes:

1.  What's at stake personally?

2.  What's at stake for relationships?

3.  What's at stake for the community?

4.  What's at stake for the Temple?

5.  What's at stake spiritually?

**Not every situation needs all 5 levels**, but knowing them helps you calibrate.

### COMMON IMPROV TRAPS

### Trap 1: Over-Planning

**Problem:** You prep so much you can't deviate

**Solution:** Prep situations and NPCs, not plots. Stay flexible.

### Trap 2: Following Your Ego

**Problem:** You want to show how clever your plot is

**Solution:** The Seekers' fun matters more than your plans. Let them shine.

### Trap 3: Saying No Too Often

**Problem:** You shut down creative ideas to maintain control

**Solution:** Default to "Yes, and" unless there's a good reason for "No"

### Trap 4: Railroading

**Problem:** You force Seekers toward your predetermined outcome

**Solution:** Present problems, not solutions. Let them find their own way.

### Trap 5: Improvising Everything

**Problem:** You prep nothing, wing it all, sessions feel aimless

**Solution:** Prep enough to have direction, then improvise within that framework
