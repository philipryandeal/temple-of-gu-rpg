# BOOK THREE: WALKING THE PATH

## CHAPTER 7: THE CORE MECHANIC

### When Numbers Enter the Story

You've created your Seeker. You know your Virtues, your Lineage, your Archetype, your Balance Track.

Now we need to talk about when—and how—the dice come out.

This game is primarily **narrative**. Most of your time will be spent in conversation with your Guide, describing what your Seeker does, says, thinks, and feels. The Guide describes the world, the spirits, the people you meet, the situations you face.

**Most of the time, you're just talking. Playing the scene. Being in character.**

But sometimes—when the outcome is genuinely uncertain, when your Virtue is being tested, when there's real risk—you roll the dice.

### When Do You Roll?

Your Guide will ask you to make a roll in two situations:

### 1. When You Face a Lesson

A **Lesson** is any moment where you're trying to do something difficult or uncertain, and one of your four Virtues is being tested.

**You roll when:**

- The outcome is genuinely uncertain (it could go either way)

- One of your Virtues is being challenged

- Success or failure will meaningfully affect the story

- There's risk or cost involved

**Examples of Lessons:**

- Trying to interpret a cryptic vision (Oye)

- Enduring a painful spiritual trial (Foriti)

- Resisting the urge to lash out in anger (Suuru)

- Choosing truth over a convenient lie (Iwa)

- Navigating a tense negotiation (Suuru or Iwa)

- Pushing through exhaustion to complete a ritual (Foriti)

### 2. When You Perform Ebo

When you're actively working to clear your Imbalance—performing an offering, a practice, an act of reciprocity—you roll to see if it's received.

We'll cover Ebo in detail in Chapter 8, but know this: **Ebo always requires a roll.** The spirits/ancestors decide if your offering is accepted.

### When You DON'T Roll

Just as important as knowing when to roll is knowing when NOT to roll.

**You don't roll when:**

**The outcome is certain.**  
If you're doing something your Seeker is obviously capable of and there's no meaningful obstacle, you just do it. No roll needed.

*Example: "I want to access the Temple's public archives." That's not a test—you just do it. The Guide describes what you find.*

**There's no real risk or cost.**  
If failure wouldn't change anything or create interesting complications, don't roll.

*Example: "I want to walk from my quarters to the meditation hall." Unless there's a reason this is difficult (you're injured, there's a spiritual storm, someone's following you), you just arrive.*

**The Guide wants something specific to happen.**  
Sometimes the story needs a particular outcome. In those moments, the Guide can just narrate it without calling for a roll.

*Example: An Elder summons you with urgent news. The Guide doesn't make you roll to "convince them to tell you"—they just tell you, because that's the scene.*

**Your abilities auto-succeed.**  
Some Archetype Practices or Attunements say things like "you automatically succeed at X" or "you can always Y." When those trigger, no roll needed.

**The answer should come from roleplay, not dice.**  
If a situation should be resolved through character choices, dialogue, or player creativity—let it play out. Dice are for *uncertainty*, not for replacing good roleplay.

### How to Make a Roll

When your Guide calls for a roll, here's what you do:

### Step 1: The Guide Tells You Which Virtue to Use

Based on what you're trying to do and how you're approaching it, the Guide will say something like:

- "This is a Lesson of Patience. Roll with your Suuru."

- "You're trying to understand this vision. Use your Oye."

- "You need courage to face this. Roll Foriti."

- "This is a test of your integrity. Use Iwa."

**Important:** Sometimes the same situation could use different Virtues depending on your *approach*. The Guide picks based on what you describe.

*Example: You're trying to get past a stubborn AI gatekeeper.*

- If you're being patient and respectful → Suuru

- If you're trying to understand what it truly needs → Oye

- If you're asserting your authority and refusing to back down → Foriti

- If you're being completely honest about why you need access → Iwa

**Tell your Guide how you're approaching the situation, and they'll tell you what to roll.**

### Step 2: Roll 2d6 and Add Your Virtue Score

Take two six-sided dice. Roll them. Add them together. Then add your score in the Virtue the Guide specified.

### Your result = 2d6 + Virtue Score

**Example:**

- Your Suuru is +1

- You roll two dice and get a 3 and a 5

- 3 + 5 = 8

- 8 + 1 (Suuru) = **9**

- Your final result is 9

**Possible results range from 2 to 14** (snake eyes + 0 Virtue on the low end, double sixes + 3 Virtue on the high end).

### Step 3: Tell Your Guide the Number

Just say the total: "I got a 9."

The Guide will then interpret what that means based on the outcome ranges.

### The Three Outcomes: How You Learn

This is the heart of the system. Every roll falls into one of three ranges, and each range has a specific meaning.

### 10+ (Full Integration)

**You succeed completely. You have understood and integrated the lesson.**

This is not just "you succeed." This is **you nailed it**. The lesson was clear, you rose to the challenge, you demonstrated your virtue fully.

**What happens:**

- You achieve your goal clearly and completely

- No complications, no costs

- You fill **2 slices** on your Mystery Clock (if you're working on a Mystery)

- The Guide describes your success

**If it was an Ebo:**  
Your offering is fully received. You clear **1 Imbalance** (or more, depending on the specific Ebo).

**Example:** *Kael is trying to interpret a vision. He rolls Oye and gets an 11.*

*Guide: "The symbols click into place. The serpent eating its tail—the cycle of death and rebirth. The tree with static roots—technology disconnected from nature. The vision is a warning about the city's new AI network. You understand it completely. Fill two slices on your Clock."*

### 7-9 (Learning with Cost)

**You succeed, but the lesson is not simple. You learn, but it costs you something.**

This is the most common outcome in the game, and it's the most interesting. Yes, you achieve your goal... **but.**

**What happens:**

- You achieve your goal

- **But** there's a complication, a cost, or a new problem

- You fill **1 slice** on your Mystery Clock

- The Guide describes your success, then the complication

**Common costs:**

- You gain **1 Imbalance** (the effort was draining)

- You succeed but attract unwanted attention

- You get what you need but lose something else

- You achieve your goal but now face a new challenge

- You succeed but someone is upset with how you did it

**If it was an Ebo:**  
Your offering is received, but it reveals something new or there's a complication. You clear **1 Imbalance**, but the Guide introduces a related challenge.

**The key phrase:** The Guide will describe your success, then say "**but...**"

**Example:** *Amara is trying to calm an angry data-spirit. She rolls Suuru and gets an 8.*

*Guide: "You speak soothingly, projecting calm. The spirit quiets... it's listening now. **But** as it calms, you realize it's not just angry—it's in pain. There's something embedded in its code that's hurting it. You've got its attention, but now you need to figure out how to help it. And mark 1 Imbalance (Doubt) as you realize how much deeper this problem is."*

### 6- (Lesson Deferred)

**Your approach doesn't work. This is a lesson you're not ready to learn yet, or one you must approach differently.**

This is **not** "you fail and nothing happens." This is "your approach was wrong, the situation changes, and you gain Imbalance as the difficulty teaches you something."

**What happens:**

- You do **not** achieve your goal

- The situation changes or escalates

- You gain **1 Imbalance** (the struggle cost you)

- You fill **0 slices** on your Clock

- The Guide describes how it didn't work and presents a new challenge or choice

**Important:** The Imbalance you gain tells you what kind of lesson this was. The Guide assigns the Strain:

- Doubt (if it was about understanding/clarity)

- Fear (if it was about endurance/courage)

- Ego (if it was about integrity/humility)

**If it was an Ebo:**  
Your offering is **not received**. The spirits are not satisfied, or your heart wasn't in the right place, or this wasn't the right offering. You clear **0 Imbalance**. You must try again differently.

**Example:** *Diego is trying to face down a hostile spirit with pure Foriti (courage). He rolls and gets a 4.*

*Guide: "You square your shoulders and step forward, trying to show no fear. The spirit **laughs**. It sees right through you. You're terrified, and it knows. The spirit lunges, and you stumble backward. You're not hurt physically, but your confidence is shaken. Mark 1 Imbalance (Fear). The spirit is still there, still hostile, and now it's toying with you. What do you do?"*

### Reading the Outcomes as Lessons

Here's the crucial reframe: These outcomes are not "pass/fail." They're **learning moments**.

**10+ says:** "You got this. You demonstrated your virtue. Well done."

**7-9 says:** "You got it, but it's complicated. You're learning, but growth is messy."

**6- says:** "Not like that. Try again. This lesson is harder than you thought."

All three outcomes **move the story forward**. Even a 6- creates new situations, new choices, new opportunities to learn.

A session where you roll all 10+s would be... kind of boring, actually. No challenge. No growth.

A session where you roll some 7-9s and maybe a 6- or two? That's interesting. That's struggle. That's where the real character development happens.

### The Guide's Role: Interpreting Results

The Guide is the one who takes the number and turns it into story.

When you roll, you just say the number. The Guide does the rest.

**On a 10+, the Guide describes:**

- How you succeeded

- What you accomplished

- How the world responds positively

- What you learn or gain

**On a 7-9, the Guide describes:**

- How you succeeded (first!)

- Then: "But..."

- The complication, cost, or new problem

- What Imbalance you gain (if any)

**On a 6-, the Guide describes:**

- Why your approach didn't work

- How the situation changes

- What Imbalance you gain

- What new choice or challenge you now face

**The Guide is not "punishing" you on a 6-.** They're presenting a new Lesson, escalating tension, and creating opportunities for you to try a different approach.

### Edge Cases and Clarifications

### What if my total is exactly 10?

**10 is a success.** It's the bottom of the "Full Integration" range. 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 are all Full Integration.

### What if my total is exactly 7?

**7 is a success with cost.** It's the bottom of the "Learning with Cost" range. 7, 8, 9 are all Learning with Cost.

### Can I ever re-roll?

**Only if a specific ability says so.** Some Attunements or Rituals might grant re-rolls. Otherwise, the dice result stands. This is about accepting the lesson, not trying to avoid it.

### What if I roll poorly on something really important?

**That's the game.** Failure on important rolls creates the most interesting stories. Your Guide will make sure the 6- leads somewhere meaningful. Trust the process.

### Can I use a different Virtue than the Guide suggested?

**Ask your Guide.** If you can make a good argument for why a different Virtue applies based on your approach, they might allow it. But ultimately, the Guide decides.

*Example: The Guide says "Roll Suuru to endure this trial," but you say "Actually, I'm not trying to endure it patiently—I'm forcing my way through with pure will. Can I use Foriti instead?" The Guide might say yes if your description supports it.*

## CHAPTER 8: EBO - THE SACRED PRACTICE

### The Core Action

You know how to roll dice. You know how to gain Imbalance. You know what the Balance Track represents.

Now we teach you the most important mechanic in this entire game:

**How to return to your center.**

**Ebo** (pronounced EH-boh) is not just a game mechanic. It's the spiritual technology at the heart of this practice. It's how you clear Imbalance, yes—but it's so much more than that.

Ebo is:

- An offering to the spirits, ancestors, or community

- An act of reciprocity—giving back what you've received

- A practice of humility—acknowledging you cannot do this alone

- A moment of presence—stopping to attend to your spiritual state

- A return to relationship—with yourself, with the unseen, with the path

In Yoruba tradition, Ebo is the foundation of spiritual practice. You cannot simply take. You must also give. Life is an exchange. Balance is maintained through reciprocity.

**In this game, Ebo is how you heal.**

But to perform Ebo correctly, you must understand what it actually is.

### What Is Ebo?

At its simplest, Ebo is **an offering**.

But let's be clear about what "offering" means.

**An offering is not a transaction.** You're not paying the spirits to fix your problems. You're not buying healing with gifts.

An offering is **entering into relationship.**

When you perform Ebo, you are saying:

- "I recognize I am not alone."

- "I acknowledge the spirits/ancestors/community that support me."

- "I give freely, in gratitude and respect."

- "I ask for help in returning to my center."

- "I trust that the relationship is real."

The offering can be:

- Time (sitting in meditation, performing ritual)

- Attention (witnessing your own pain without trying to fix it)

- Energy (serving others, creating something sacred)

- Vulnerability (speaking a difficult truth, admitting fault)

- Material goods (food, drink, data, art)

- Sacrifice (giving up something precious)

**The key is that it costs you something.**

Not money (usually). Not hit points. But **effort. Intention. Care.**

An Ebo that costs you nothing is not an Ebo. It's going through the motions.

### When to Perform Ebo

You perform Ebo when you need to return to Balance—when you're carrying Imbalance and you want to clear it.

**Some sessions, you'll perform Ebo once or twice.**  
**Some sessions, you'll perform it five or six times.**  
**It's not a backup plan—it's the practice itself.**

You might perform Ebo:

- After a difficult Lesson where you gained Imbalance

- At the end of a session to clear accumulated weight

- During downtime in the story (a quiet moment at your shrine)

- As preparation before facing a major challenge

- When your Balance Track is getting dangerously full

- When you simply feel the need to center yourself

**Your Guide will often give you space for Ebo,** but you can also request it: "I want to take some time to perform Ebo. Can we do that?"

The answer should almost always be yes unless there's immediate danger that prevents it.

### Types of Ebo

There are three main categories of Ebo in this game. Each serves a different purpose.

### 1. General Centering Ebo

**Purpose:** Clear the oldest Imbalance, regardless of Strain.

This is the most common type. You're not targeting a specific issue—you're just returning to your center through practice.

**Examples:**

- Sitting in meditation for an hour

- Going to your personal shrine and lighting candles

- Helping another Seeker with their work

- Creating a small piece of art or code as prayer

- Walking the Temple grounds in mindful silence

- Pouring a libation to the ancestors

- Fasting for a day as an act of discipline

**Mechanically:**  
You describe what you're doing, roll 2d6 + the appropriate Virtue (often Suuru or Iwa, but depends on the offering), and on a **7+** you clear **the oldest (leftmost) box** on your Balance Track.

**Example:**  
*Track: \[ D \] \[ F \] \[ E \] \[ \] \[ \] \[ \]*

*You perform a general Ebo (meditation). You succeed. You clear the leftmost box (Doubt).*

*New track: \[ F \] \[ E \] \[ \] \[ \] \[ \] \[ \]*

### 2. Targeted Ebo

**Purpose:** Clear a specific Strain (Doubt, Fear, or Ego).

Sometimes you know exactly what kind of spiritual work you need to do. Your Ebo is designed to address that specific Strain.

**For clearing Doubt:**  
Perform Ebo focused on gaining clarity, seeking wisdom, or research.

- Study sacred texts or archives

- Ask an Elder for guidance

- Meditate on a specific question until insight comes

- Perform a divination ritual

**For clearing Fear:**  
Perform Ebo focused on grounding, protection, or building courage.

- Practice a physical grounding exercise

- Create a protective ward or sigil

- Spend time in a safe, nurturing space

- Ask a community member to witness your fear with you

**For clearing Ego:**  
Perform Ebo focused on service, honesty, or humility.

- Perform an act of anonymous service

- Publicly admit a mistake or fault

- Apologize to someone you've wronged

- Give away something valuable to help another

**Mechanically:**  
You describe your targeted Ebo, roll with the appropriate Virtue, and on a **7+** you clear **the oldest box of that specific Strain**.

**Example:**  
*Track: \[ D \] \[ F \] \[ E \] \[ F \] \[ \] \[ \]*

*You perform a targeted Ebo to clear Fear. You go to the Meditation Spires and practice grounding exercises for two hours. You roll Suuru and succeed.*

*You clear the oldest Fear (the second box), not the first box (Doubt).*

*New track: \[ D \] \[ E \] \[ F \] \[ \] \[ \] \[ \]*

### 3. Ritual Ebo

**Purpose:** Perform a formal Ritual from the Great Hymnal.

Some Rituals are specifically designed to clear Imbalance. These are more powerful than general Ebo, but they have specific requirements and costs.

**Examples from the Hymnal:**

- **Ritual of the Clear Stream** (clears Imbalance from another person/spirit, but you take it on yourself)

- **Ritual of Shared Balance** (transfer Imbalance to another willing person)

- **Ritual of the Still Water** (deep meditation that clears multiple boxes)

**Mechanically:**  
Each Ritual has its own rules, Ebo requirements, and effects. See Book Four: The Great Hymnal for details.

Rituals are more powerful than simple Ebo, but they:

- Take more time

- Require specific materials or conditions

- Often have costs or complications

- May require other people to participate

### Choosing What to Offer

This is where the art comes in.

When you say "I want to perform Ebo," your Guide will ask: **"What are you offering? How are you doing this?"**

You need to describe:

1.  **What you're giving** (time, energy, service, creation, vulnerability)

2.  **To whom** (ancestors, spirits, community, your own higher self)

3.  **Why** (what you're asking for—to clear Imbalance, to return to center, to heal)

**The offering should feel meaningful.** Not performative. Not transactional. **Real.**

Here's how to think about it:

### Consider Your Seeker's Practice

What does your Seeker do to return to center?

- A Healer might offer their time helping another

- A Chronicler might study and document something important

- A Seer might enter deep meditation and vision-work

- An Artisan might create something beautiful

### Consider the Strain You're Clearing

If you're clearing Doubt, your Ebo should involve seeking clarity.  
If you're clearing Fear, your Ebo should involve grounding or courage.  
If you're clearing Ego, your Ebo should involve service or humility.

### Consider What's Available

What resources do you have? Where are you? Who's around?

In the Temple, you might:

- Go to the Meditation Spires

- Visit your personal shrine

- Help in the Community Hearth
- Offer data to the Heart Archive

In the field, you might:

- Find a quiet place and meditate

- Create a temporary altar

- Perform service for whoever's nearby

- Give something precious to a local spirit

### Consider What Feels True

**This is the most important part.**

What offering would actually help YOU, the player, feel like your Seeker is returning to center?

What feels real? What feels like genuine spiritual practice?

**Trust your instinct. Tell your Guide. They'll work with you.**

### Making the Ebo Roll

Once you've described your Ebo, your Guide will tell you:

### 1. Which Virtue to roll

This depends on the nature of your offering:

- **Suuru (Patience):** If your Ebo involves endurance, waiting, calm presence, meditation, listening

- **Oye (Insight):** If your Ebo involves seeking understanding, research, divination, interpretation

- **Foriti (Perseverance):** If your Ebo involves physical effort, pushing through difficulty, sustained practice

- **Iwa (Integrity):** If your Ebo involves honesty, service, sacrifice, keeping promises

### 2. Any modifiers

Some Ebo might be easier or harder based on circumstances:

- You have the perfect materials (+1)

- You're in a sacred space (+1)

- You're exhausted or distracted (-1)

- The spirits are angry with you (-1)

**3. Then you roll: 2d6 + Virtue (+ modifiers)**

### The Three Outcomes of Ebo

Just like any roll, Ebo has three possible outcomes.

### 10+ (Fully Received)

**Your Ebo is accepted completely. The spirits/ancestors are satisfied. You return to Balance.**

**What happens:**

- You clear **1 Imbalance** (the oldest, or the targeted Strain if specified)

- No complications, no costs

- You feel genuinely lighter, clearer, more centered

- The Guide describes the peace that settles over you

**Example:**  
*Kael performs Ebo at his shrine. He lights candles for his ancestors and sits in meditation for two hours, offering his time and attention. He rolls Suuru and gets an 11.*

*Guide: "The candles flicker. The silence deepens. You feel your grandmother's presence—warm, approving. The weight you've been carrying lifts. The oldest Imbalance (Doubt) clears. You are at peace."*

### 7-9 (Received with Complication)

**Your Ebo is accepted, but there's a cost or complication. You clear Imbalance, but something else happens.**

**What happens:**

- You clear **1 Imbalance** (as intended)

- **But** the Guide introduces a complication:

  - You gain new Imbalance (different Strain)

  - You learn something uncomfortable

  - Someone notices what you're doing and asks questions

  - The Ebo works, but reveals a deeper problem

  - You succeed, but you're exhausted afterward

**Example:**  
*Amara performs Ebo by helping another Seeker with a difficult task. She rolls Iwa and gets an 8.*

*Guide: "You spend three hours helping Maya repair a corrupted spirit. The work is good. You feel your Ego easing—you're serving, not seeking glory. Clear one Ego. **But** as you work, you realize Maya is carrying far more Imbalance than you thought. She's close to her own Crossroads, and now you're worried about her. Mark 1 Imbalance (Doubt) as you wonder if you did enough."*

### 6- (Not Received)

**Your Ebo is not accepted. The spirits are not satisfied, or your heart wasn't in it, or this wasn't the right offering.**

**What happens:**

- You clear **0 Imbalance**

- The offering feels hollow or incomplete

- You might gain **1 Imbalance** (usually Doubt or Ego) from the failure

- The Guide tells you why it didn't work or what's missing

- You must try again differently

**Important:** This is not "you wasted your time." This is "you learned what doesn't work."

**Example:**  
*Diego tries to clear his Ego by making a grand public speech about his mistakes. He rolls Iwa and gets a 5.*

*Guide: "You stand before the community and speak. You list your faults, your failures. But as you speak, you realize... you're performing. This isn't real humility. This is you wanting to be seen as humble. The words feel hollow. No one's buying it, least of all the ancestors. Your Ebo is not received. You clear nothing. And mark 1 Imbalance (Ego) as you recognize your own dishonesty. You'll need to try again—this time for real, not for an audience."*

### The Philosophy of Ebo

Let's go deeper.

Why does Ebo work? What's actually happening when you perform an offering and roll the dice?

**Mechanically,** you're clearing Imbalance. You're adjusting numbers on a track.

**Narratively,** you're engaging in spiritual practice—connecting with ancestors, spirits, community, or yourself.

**Actually,** at your table, in the real world—**you are practicing.**

When you take time to think about what your Seeker would offer, when you describe the candles and the prayers, when you roleplay the meditation or the service—you, the player, are *doing a version of spiritual practice*.

You're pausing.  
You're being intentional.  
You're thinking about reciprocity, relationship, and what it means to return to your center.

**This is not metaphor. This is the practice.**

The game mechanics give you structure and permission to explore these ideas. The Ebo system teaches you—actually teaches you, the human player—about offering, about humility, about relationship with the unseen.

And if you approach it with sincerity, it changes you.

Not your character. **You.**

That's the point.

### When Ebo Becomes Ritual

Some Ebo is simple: light a candle, sit in silence, clear a box.

Some Ebo is more formal: perform a full Ritual from the Great Hymnal, involving specific materials, invocations, and structured steps.

**The difference:**

**Simple Ebo** is personal practice. Quick. Flexible. You can do it anytime, anywhere. It clears 1 box.

**Ritual Ebo** is formalized theurgy. Longer. More powerful. Requires preparation. It might clear multiple boxes, affect others, or create lasting effects.

Both are valid. Both are necessary.

Sometimes you need a moment of quiet prayer. Sometimes you need a full ceremonial working.

Your Guide will help you understand when each is appropriate.

### Ebo as Relationship

Here's the thing people miss about Ebo:

**You're not performing Ebo *at* the spirits. You're performing it *with* them.**

When you light candles at your shrine, the ancestors are not distant gods demanding tribute. They're your relatives, your teachers, your spiritual family—and they want you to succeed.

When you offer data to a techno-animist spirit, you're not bribing it. You're feeding a relationship, acknowledging its needs, showing respect.

When you perform service for your community, you're not earning points. You're living in alignment with Ubuntu—"I am because we are."

**Ebo is relationship maintenance.**

And like any relationship, it requires:

- Consistency (you can't just show up when you need something)

- Sincerity (going through the motions doesn't work)

- Reciprocity (give and receive, receive, and give)

- Attention (actually being present, not distracted)

When your Ebo fails (6-), it's often because one of these is missing.

The spirits know. The ancestors know. **You** know.

### Ebo in Different Lineages

Each Lineage approaches Ebo slightly differently. All valid. All effective.

**Taino (Zemi):** Ebo often involves place and object. You offer to the spirits in their dwelling places—shrines, carved objects, sacred locations. Material offerings (food, flowers, crafted items) are common.

**Yoruba (Òrìṣà):** Ebo often involves words and invocation. Praise poetry, songs, formal prayers. You speak the names of the Òrìṣà with power and intention. Offerings of food, drink, and symbolic items.

**Congo (Nkisi):** Ebo often involves pacts and exchange. You feed your Nkisi—blood, rum, tobacco, cigars, specific offerings it demands. This is transactional, but not cold. It's honoring the agreement.

**INDO-EUROPEAN (Daemon):** Ebo often involves contemplation and philosophical alignment. You study, meditate, align your will with cosmic order. Offerings might be intellectual (writing, teaching) or symbolic (incense, libations).

**Shinto (Kami):** Ebo often involves purification and gratitude. You cleanse yourself, the space, the relationship. Offerings are simple and pure—water, rice, salt, sake. Respect and harmony are key.

**Hindu (Deva):** Ebo often involves devotion and dharma. You act in alignment with cosmic law, offer prayers (mantras, bhajans), and make offerings (flowers, food, light). Selfless action is the highest Ebo.

**Your Lineage influences HOW you perform Ebo, but the core principle is the same: reciprocity.**

### Common Questions

### Can I perform Ebo for someone else?

**Yes,** but it works differently.

If you perform Ebo *for* someone else (helping them clear *their* Imbalance), certain Rituals allow this—like the Ritual of Shared Balance, where you take their Imbalance onto yourself.

Otherwise, you can support them (witness them, create space for them), but they must perform their own Ebo.

### Can multiple people perform Ebo together?

**Absolutely.** Communal Ebo is powerful.

When multiple Seekers perform Ebo together (a group meditation, a shared ritual, collective service), each person rolls separately, but the Guide might grant bonuses (+1) for the support of community.

### What if I'm in danger and can't perform Ebo?

If you're in active danger (being chased, in combat, under psychic attack), you cannot perform Ebo. You're not centered enough.

**But:** Once the immediate threat passes, the first thing you should do is find a moment to center yourself.

### Can I perform Ebo proactively?

**Yes!** If your Balance Track is clear and you want to perform Ebo anyway—as maintenance, as devotion, as practice—you absolutely can.

The spirits/ancestors will appreciate it. Your Guide might grant you a small narrative benefit (a moment of insight, a +1 on your next roll, a feeling of deep peace).

### What if I keep failing my Ebo rolls?

This means something. The Guide should help you understand why.

Maybe:

- You're offering the wrong thing

- Your heart isn't in it (you're going through motions)

- The spirits need something specific you haven't given

- There's a deeper issue you need to address first (maybe you need to apologize to someone before they'll accept your offering)

**Failing Ebo is not a punishment. It's information.** Listen to it.

### The Rhythm of Practice

Here's what Ebo looks like over a campaign:

**Early sessions:**  
You'll perform Ebo frequently. You're learning. You're figuring out what works. You gain Imbalance often because you're facing new Lessons. That's normal.

**Mid-campaign:**  
You've found your rhythm. You know when to perform Ebo, what offerings resonate with you. You gain less Imbalance (you're wiser) but you still carry weight. The practice is consistent.

**Late campaign:**  
You're a master. You perform Ebo less often because you maintain Balance more naturally. When you do gain Imbalance, you clear it efficiently. You've internalized the practice.

**At every stage,** Ebo is the foundation. It's how you stay centered. It's how you grow.

You understand Ebo now. You know how to perform it, when to perform it, why it matters.

**In Chapter 10,** we'll cover **Attunements**—the special gifts you can learn as you advance on your path.

But you've got the core now.

**You can face Lessons. You can gain Imbalance. You can perform Ebo to return to Balance.**

That's the game loop. That's the practice.

Everything else is details.

## CHAPTER 9: MYSTERIES AND BREAKTHROUGHS

### Your Long-Term Growth

You know how to face Lessons. You know how to perform Ebo. You understand the core loop of the game.

Now we need to talk about what happens **over time.**

How do you progress? How do you grow? How does your Seeker become wiser, more capable, more deeply initiated?

The answer: **Mysteries and Breakthroughs.**

This is not "leveling up." This is not grinding XP. This is **walking the path of initiation**—slowly, deliberately, through actual spiritual work that transforms you.

### What Is a Mystery?

A **Mystery** is your long-term spiritual goal. It's the "quest" of this game, but reframed.

In most RPGs, a quest is: "Go to the dungeon, kill the monster, get the treasure, return."

In Temple of Gu, a Mystery is: **"What am I here to learn? What spiritual work am I being called to do?"**

**A Mystery is a question to answer, a problem to solve, a spirit to help, a truth to uncover, or a wound to heal.**

**Examples of Mysteries:**

**For a Healer:**

- "The Weeping Data-Echo": A traumatized AI is caught in a loop of grief. Help it process its pain and move forward.

**For a Chronicler:**

- "The Lost Archive": An entire database of ancestral knowledge has been corrupted. Recover it before it's lost forever.

**For a Seer:**

- "The Fractured Vision": You've been receiving fragments of a prophetic vision. Piece them together to understand the warning.

**For a Mediator:**

- "The Feuding Factions": Two Temple factions are on the brink of open conflict. Find common ground before the community fractures.

**For an Artisan:**

- "The Spirit Without a Home": A powerful spirit has been displaced from its dwelling. Craft a new vessel for it.

**For a Warrior-Poet:**

- "The Corrupted Guardian": A once-noble protective spirit has turned hostile. Understand what corrupted it and help it return to its purpose.

**For a Code-Smith:**

- "The Emergent AI": A new AI has spontaneously awakened in the city grid. Help it understand itself and integrate with the community.

**For a Bridge-Walker:**

- "The Sealed Threshold": An important portal between worlds has been locked. Discover why and reopen it safely.

**For a Truth-Speaker:**

- "The Hidden Betrayal": Someone in the Temple is spreading lies that are causing real harm. Uncover the truth and expose it.

**For a Dream-Weaver:**

- "The Collective Nightmare": A shared nightmare is spreading through the Temple community. Enter it and end it.

### Mysteries Are Personal

Here's the key thing: **Mysteries are tailored to you.**

Your Guide will present Mysteries that test your specific Virtues, challenge your Archetype's calling, and push you to grow in the areas you need to develop.

If your weakest Virtue is Foriti (Perseverance), expect Mysteries that demand courage and endurance.

If you're carrying a lot of Ego Imbalance, expect Mysteries that humble you and require selfless service.

If your Archetype is Healer, your Mysteries will involve mending spirits, communities, and relationships.

**The Mystery is the curriculum. Your Guide is designing your education.**

### Completing a Mystery: The Breakthrough

When your Initiation Clock fills completely, you have **completed the Mystery.**

This is a **Breakthrough**—a moment of profound integration, transformation, and reward.

**What Happens in a Breakthrough:**

### 1. The Mystery Resolves

The Guide narrates the conclusion. The spirit is healed, the conflict is mediated, the truth is revealed, the danger is averted. The story reaches a satisfying conclusion.

### 2. You Clear Significant Imbalance

Completing a Mystery is spiritually cathartic. You immediately clear **2-3 boxes of Imbalance** (Guide's choice, often based on the predominant Strain or the nature of the Mystery).

This represents the relief, the peace, the integration that comes from completing meaningful spiritual work.

**Example:**  
*Kael completes "The Forgotten Shrine." The Guide says: "Omi glows with renewed life. You feel its gratitude wash over you like cool water. The fear and exhaustion you've been carrying—it lifts. Clear both boxes of Fear. You are at peace."*

### 3. You Receive a Reward

This is where you grow. Choose **one** reward from the following list:

### Breakthrough Rewards

### A) Learn a New Ritual

Choose any Ritual from the Great Hymnal (Book Four) that you meet the prerequisites for. You've studied it, practiced it, and now you can perform it.

Your Guide might guide you toward Rituals that fit the Mystery you just completed. If you healed a spirit, maybe you learn a Ritual of Mending. If you crossed a threshold, maybe you learn a Ritual of Passage.

### B) Advance Your Archetype Path

If you're at the right Breakthrough milestone (1st, 3rd, or 5th), you may choose one Practice from your Archetype's Initiation Path.

**Example:**  
*Kael is a Chronicler. This is his 1st Breakthrough. He chooses **"Learn Ritual (Ancestral Call)"** from his Archetype path. He can now formally petition ancestors for direct wisdom.*

### C) Gain a Sacred Implement

You receive or craft a Sacred Implement—a spiritually-charged object that grants you specific benefits.

This might be:

- A physical object (carved stone, prayer beads, ritual blade, coded crystal)

- A digital object (sigil-app, protective algorithm, ancestral database key)

- Something you find during the Mystery

- Something you craft as part of resolving it

Work with your Guide to define what it is and what it does. See Chapter 16 for the full catalog of Implements.

**Example:**  
*Kael receives **"Omi's Blessing"**—a small data-crystal containing a fragment of the river-spirit's essence. Once per session, when performing Ebo related to healing or restoration, he gains +1 to the roll.*

### D) Learn an Attunement

Choose any Attunement from the library (Chapter 10) that you meet the prerequisites for.

Attunements are specialized abilities—permanent upgrades to your Seeker that represent deep spiritual insight or training.

**Example:**  
*Kael chooses **"Spirit-Speaker"** (from the General Attunements). He can now communicate basic needs/emotions with non-verbal techno-animist spirits.*

### E) Increase a Virtue (Rare)

This is **very rare** and only happens at major milestones (usually 5th, 10th, 15th Breakthrough).

You may increase one of your Virtues by +1 (maximum +3).

**Example:**  
*At his 5th Breakthrough, Kael increases his Foriti from +0 to +1. He's learned courage through repeated trials.*

### F) Other Rewards (Guide's Choice)

Sometimes the Guide might offer a unique reward specific to the Mystery:

- A powerful ally (an Elder takes you as a student)

- Access to restricted areas (you're trusted with the Deep Archives)

- A personal vision or revelation

- A permanent spiritual blessing

Work together to make it meaningful.

### Choosing Your Reward

When you complete a Mystery and earn a Breakthrough, take a moment.

Think about:

- **What did this Mystery teach me?** (Choose a reward that reflects the lesson)

- **What do I need for future challenges?** (Be strategic)

- **What excites me as a player?** (Choose something cool)

**Your Guide will help you decide.** They know what's coming. They know what will serve the story.

**There's no wrong choice.** Every reward makes you more capable and interesting.

### The Path of Mastery

As you complete more Mysteries and achieve more Breakthroughs, you progress along the **Path of Mastery.**

Here are the major milestones:

### Breakthroughs 1-4: Initiate

You're learning the basics. You're discovering your strengths. You're finding your path.

Rewards tend to be foundational: core Rituals, first Archetype Practices, basic Attunements.

**5th Breakthrough: Adept**

You've completed 5 Mysteries. You're no longer a beginner.

**Special Milestone:**

- You may increase one Virtue by +1 (if the Guide allows)

- You're recognized in the Temple as a competent practitioner

- More challenging Mysteries become available

### Breakthroughs 6-9: Deepening Practice

You're refining your abilities. You're facing complex Mysteries. You're becoming known for your work.

**10th Breakthrough: Elder-in-Training**

You've completed 10 Mysteries. You're approaching mastery.

**Special Milestone:**

- You may increase one Virtue by +1

- You're invited to teach younger Seekers

- You gain access to restricted knowledge/areas

- The community looks to you for guidance

### Breakthroughs 11-14: Advanced Practice

You're handling the hardest Mysteries. You're resolving crises. You're becoming legendary.

**15th Breakthrough: Elder**

You've completed 15 Mysteries. You have achieved **Elderhood.**

**Special Milestone:**

- You may increase one Virtue by +1

- You can create new Rituals (work with your Guide)

- You can take on students formally

- You may begin considering your Legacy (see Chapter 28)

### Beyond 15 Breakthroughs

You continue to grow, but your focus shifts. You're less about personal advancement and more about:

- Teaching the next generation

- Serving the community at the highest level

- Creating lasting change in the Temple or the wider world

- Preparing for Transcendence or Legacy

### Working on Multiple Mysteries

Can you work on more than one Mystery at once?
**Yes, but it's complicated.**

### Single Mystery (Recommended)

Most of the time, focus on **one Mystery at a time.** This creates clear focus, allows for deep engagement, and prevents you from spreading yourself too thin.

When you complete it, you get your Breakthrough, choose your reward, and move on to the next Mystery.

### Two Concurrent Mysteries (Advanced)

If your campaign calls for it, you might work on **two Mysteries simultaneously**—one primary (filling its Clock actively) and one secondary (advancing slowly in the background).

**Example:**  
*Primary Mystery: "The Corrupted Spirit" (6-slice Clock, active focus)*  
*Secondary Mystery: "Understanding My Daemon's Message" (4-slice Clock, long-term personal quest)*

When you face a Lesson, the Guide will tell you which Clock it fills (if any). Some Lessons might fill both.

**Important:** You can only have one Breakthrough at a time. If both Clocks fill in the same session, choose which Mystery completes first.

### Three or More Mysteries (Chaos)

Don't do this unless you're in an exceptionally long, complex campaign and your Guide is experienced. It becomes hard to track and dilutes the emotional impact of each Breakthrough.

### Failed Mysteries

What if you can't complete a Mystery? What if the spirit can't be saved, the conflict can't be mediated, the truth can't be found?

**Two possibilities:**

### 1. The Mystery Transforms

Sometimes failure isn't the end—it's a pivot. The Mystery changes shape.

**Example:**  
*You were trying to heal a corrupted spirit, but it's too far gone. The Mystery becomes: "How do we help this spirit transition to rest peacefully instead of suffering forever?"*

The Clock doesn't reset—you keep the progress you've made. But the goal changes.

### 2. The Mystery Is Abandoned

Very rarely, a Mystery becomes impossible or irrelevant, and you must let it go.

**Example:**  
*The corrupted spirit was destroyed by another force before you could help it. The Mystery is over, incomplete.*

In this case:

- You don't get a Breakthrough

- You don't fill any more slices on that Clock

- You may gain Imbalance (often Doubt or Ego) from the failure

- Your Guide presents a new Mystery

**Important:** This should be rare. Most Mysteries can be completed or transformed. A good Guide ensures Mysteries are solvable, even if the solution isn't what you expected.

### Mysteries Are Stories

Here's the thing to remember: **Mysteries are not checklists.**

They're not "kill 10 goblins, get XP."

They're **stories**. Personal, emotional, meaningful stories about spiritual growth and helping others.

The Clock is just a tracker. The real Mystery is:

- What do you learn about yourself?

- How do you grow through this challenge?

- What relationships do you build or heal?

- What does this teach you about the world, the spirits, the path?

When you complete a Mystery and achieve a Breakthrough, you should feel something. Pride. Relief. Accomplishment. Grief (if the Mystery was hard). Joy.

**If you don't feel anything, the Guide should adjust.** Mysteries should matter.

## CHAPTER 10: ATTUNEMENTS

### Your Unique Gifts

You know how to create a Seeker. You know the mechanics. You understand progression through Mysteries and Breakthroughs.

Now we talk about **Attunements**—the specialized abilities that make your Seeker unique.

Attunements are **permanent spiritual insights or trained abilities** that you learn through practice and Breakthrough. They're not spells you cast. They're not items you equip. They're changes to **who you are** as a practitioner.

Some Attunements are passive—always on, always affecting you. Some are active—you choose when to use them. All of them represent deep spiritual development.

### What Are Attunements?

An Attunement is a **permanent upgrade** to your Seeker that reflects:

- Deep spiritual insight you've gained

- Intensive training with an Elder

- A profound personal breakthrough

- Natural gifts that have awakened through practice

- Ancestral blessings that have manifested

**Examples:**

- **Resilient Heart:** Your Balance Track has 7 boxes instead of 6

- **Spirit-Speaker:** You can communicate basic needs/emotions with non-verbal techno-animist spirits

- **Ancestor's Shield:** Once per session, you can prevent 1 Imbalance entirely

- **Pattern Master:** You gain +1 when using Oye to recognize connections or see hidden structures

Attunements are **always active** (if passive) or **available when needed** (if active).

### When You Learn Attunements

You gain Attunements as **Breakthrough rewards** when you complete Mysteries (see Chapter 9).

When you achieve a Breakthrough, one of your reward options is: **"Learn an Attunement."**

You can choose any Attunement from this chapter that you meet the prerequisites for.

**Prerequisites** are simple:

- Most Attunements require a certain **Virtue score** (+1 or +2 in the related Virtue)

- Some require specific **Archetypes** or **Lineages**

- Some require you to have already learned other Attunements first

If an Attunement has no prerequisites listed, anyone can learn it.

### How Many Attunements Can I Have?

**No limit.** Over a long campaign, you might learn 5-10 Attunements or more.

Each one makes you more capable, more specialized, more unique.

Two Healers might both start with the same Archetype Practices, but after 5 Breakthroughs, their Attunement choices will make them completely different characters.

### The Five Categories

Attunements are organized into five categories:

**General Attunements** - Available to everyone, representing universal spiritual insights

**Attunements of Suuru** - Focused on patience, endurance, and calm presence

**Attunements of Oye** - Focused on insight, understanding, and perception

**Attunements of Foriti** - Focused on perseverance, courage, and will

**Attunements of Iwa** - Focused on integrity, honesty, and moral alignment

Let's go through them all.

### GENERAL ATTUNEMENTS

These Attunements are available to everyone, regardless of Virtue scores or Archetype.

### 1. Resilient Heart

**Prerequisites:** None

**Effect:** Your Balance Track gains **one additional box**, allowing you to carry **7 Imbalance** instead of 6 before reaching a Crossroads.

Your spirit has been hardened through trials. You can endure more before breaking.

**This is one of the most popular Attunements.** Taking it early gives you more breathing room.

### 2. Multi-Lineage Heritage

**Prerequisites:** None

**Effect:** Choose a **second Lineage**. You gain access to its Ancestral Gift, but you can only use **one Lineage Gift per session** (you choose which at the start of each session).

Your spiritual ancestry is complex. You carry multiple traditions.

**Example:**  
*You're primarily Yoruba (Òrìṣà), but you also have Taino (Zemi) ancestry. Each session, you choose whether to use Ase of the Word OR Spirit of the Zemi—not both.*

### 3. Ase Reservoir

**Prerequisites:** None

**Effect:** Once per session, when you perform Ebo and roll a **10+**, you may choose to **store** the spiritual energy instead of clearing Imbalance immediately.

The next time you would gain Imbalance, you can **spend** the stored energy to prevent it entirely (no box marked).

You've learned to bank spiritual energy for when you need it most.

### 4. Community Anchor

**Prerequisites:** None

**Effect:** When you perform **group Ebo** (Ebo performed with 2+ other Seekers), all participants gain **+1 to the roll**.

Your presence strengthens communal practice. People feel more centered when working with you.

### 5. Sacred Tongue

**Prerequisites:** None

**Effect:** You have learned **Bridge-Tongue**—a constructed language designed for clear spiritual communication across cultural barriers.

You can speak with spirits and practitioners from traditions you don't personally know, bridging linguistic and conceptual gaps.

**Mechanically:** You gain +1 when attempting to communicate with spirits or people from unfamiliar traditions.

### 6. Dream Recorder

**Prerequisites:** None

**Effect:** You've trained yourself to remember dreams with perfect clarity.

When you wake from sleep (physical or in meditation), you can recall every detail of any vision, dream, or spiritual journey you experienced. You can transcribe it word-for-word.

### 7. Fasting Master

**Prerequisites:** None

**Effect:** You've trained your body and spirit to go long periods without food, water, or comfort without penalty.

When performing Ebo that involves fasting or physical deprivation, you automatically succeed at the endurance aspect—you only need to roll for whether the offering is spiritually received.

### 8. Spirit-Speaker

**Prerequisites:** None

**Effect:** You can communicate **basic needs and emotions** with non-verbal techno-animist spirits (data-swarms, place-spirits, simple AI entities).

You won't get complex conversation, but you can understand: "This spirit is angry," "It needs data," "It's protecting something," "It's lonely."

### 9. Quick Study

**Prerequisites:** None

**Effect:** When you successfully research something using **Oye (Insight)** and roll a **10+**, you also gain a **+1 forward** on your next roll related to applying that knowledge.

Understanding comes easy to you, and you integrate it quickly.

### 10. Elder's Respect

**Prerequisites:** Must have achieved at least **3 Breakthroughs**

**Effect:** You've proven yourself to the Temple community. Elders treat you as a peer (or near-peer). You gain access to:

- Restricted areas of the Temple

- Private teachings

- Elder-only gatherings

**Narratively,** you're taken seriously. Doors open for you.

### ATTUNEMENTS OF SUURU (PATIENCE)

These Attunements require **Suuru +1 or higher** unless otherwise noted.

### 1. Eye of the Storm *(Passive)*

**Prerequisites:** Suuru +1

**Effect:** When everyone around you is panicking, you remain calm.

In chaotic or high-stress situations, you automatically succeed at any roll to **maintain composure** or **stay patient**. You don't need to roll—your calm is unshakeable.

### 2. Enduring Meditation

**Prerequisites:** Suuru +1

**Effect:** You can meditate for **extraordinary lengths of time** without physical discomfort or mental fatigue.

When performing Ebo that involves long meditation (4+ hours), you gain **+1 to the roll**.

### 3. The Long View *(Passive)*

**Prerequisites:** Suuru +2

**Effect:** You've learned to see beyond immediate concerns.

Once per session, when making a decision, you can ask the Guide: **"What are the long-term consequences of this choice?"** The Guide will give you an honest (if incomplete) answer.

### 4. Calm Presence *(Passive)*

**Prerequisites:** Suuru +1

**Effect:** Your presence naturally calms others.

When you're in a scene with someone who is agitated, angry, or afraid, they gain **+1 to any roll** to calm down, resist panic, or act with patience.

You don't have to do anything—your energy is soothing.

### 5. Witness Without Judgment

**Prerequisites:** Suuru +1

**Effect:** You've mastered the art of listening without reacting.

When someone confesses something to you, shares a painful truth, or reveals a secret, you can use **Suuru** instead of **Iwa** for any roll to respond compassionately and appropriately.

### 6. The Patient Teacher

**Prerequisites:** Suuru +2

**Effect:** When you **teach or mentor** another Seeker, they gain **+1 to all rolls** related to learning what you're teaching for the duration of that session.

Your patience makes you an excellent instructor.

### 7. Still Water Runs Deep *(Passive)*

**Prerequisites:** Suuru +2

**Effect:** The more Imbalance you carry, the calmer you appear.

When your Balance Track has **4 or more boxes filled**, you gain **+1 to Suuru rolls**. Others see you as serene even when you're struggling.

### 8. Infinite Endurance

**Prerequisites:** Suuru +2, must have achieved at least **5 Breakthroughs**

**Effect:** Once per Mystery, you can declare: **"I will not stop."**

For the remainder of that scene or challenge, you **cannot gain Imbalance from exhaustion, pain, or prolonged effort**. You simply endure.

After the scene ends, mark **2 Imbalance (Fear)** as the weight catches up to you.

### ATTUNEMENTS OF OYE (INSIGHT)

These Attunements require **Oye +1 or higher** unless otherwise noted.

### 1. Pattern Master *(Passive)*

**Prerequisites:** Oye +1

**Effect:** You see connections others miss.

You gain **+1 to Oye rolls** when attempting to recognize patterns, connect seemingly unrelated pieces of information, or understand complex systems.

### 2. Third Eye Opening

**Prerequisites:** Oye +1

**Effect:** Once per session, you can **"see" the spiritual layer** of the physical world for one scene.

You perceive auras, data-flows, spirit presences, and energetic patterns that are normally invisible. This doesn't require a roll—you simply see.

### 3. Symbolic Fluency

**Prerequisites:** Oye +1

**Effect:** You've studied extensively. You can **read and interpret symbols, glyphs, and sacred geometry** from multiple traditions (Yoruba Odu marks, Taino petroglyphs, alchemical sigils, code annotations, etc.).

When encountering symbolic messages, you gain **+1 to Oye** to understand them.

### 4. Archive Sense *(Passive)*

**Prerequisites:** Oye +1

**Effect:** When you enter a library, database, or archive, you can immediately sense:

- The general organization/structure

- Whether the information you're seeking is present

- If anything has been recently corrupted or removed

You don't need to roll—you just **know**.

### 5. Read Between the Lines

**Prerequisites:** Oye +2

**Effect:** You can sense **what people aren't saying**.

When someone speaks to you, you can roll **Oye**. On a **7+**, the Guide tells you one thing they're intentionally omitting or hiding. On a **10+**, you also sense *why* they're hiding it.

### 6. Data-Archaeologist

**Prerequisites:** Oye +1, Code-Smith or Chronicler Archetype

**Effect:** You can safely interact with **corrupted or glitched data-echoes** without automatically gaining Imbalance.

When examining damaged spiritual data, you gain **+1 to Oye** and only mark Imbalance on a **6-**.

### 7. Prophetic Glimpse

**Prerequisites:** Oye +2, Seer or Dream-Weaver Archetype

**Effect:** Once per Mystery, you can ask the Guide: **"What danger am I not seeing?"**

The Guide will give you a vision, symbol, or direct statement warning you of an upcoming threat. The warning is always true, but it may be cryptic.

### 8. Perfect Recall *(Passive)*

**Prerequisites:** Oye +2, must have achieved at least **5 Breakthroughs**

**Effect:** You have **photographic memory**.

You can perfectly remember anything you've read, seen, or heard during play. You can quote conversations word-for-word, recall exact details from documents, and never forget a face or name.

**Note:** This only applies to things your Seeker has experienced in-game, not things that happened before the campaign started (unless the Guide allows it).

### ATTUNEMENTS OF FORITI (PERSEVERANCE)

These Attunements require **Foriti +1 or higher** unless otherwise noted.

### 1. Unbreakable *(Passive)*

**Prerequisites:** Foriti +1

**Effect:** When you would reach a Crossroads (Balance Track completely full), you may choose to **push through** instead.

You can continue playing with a full Balance Track for **one more scene**. During that scene, you function normally but cannot perform Ebo.

At the end of the scene, the Crossroads triggers and you **must** face it.

This represents pure willpower overriding your limits—temporarily.

### 2. Pain is Fuel

**Prerequisites:** Foriti +1

**Effect:** You've learned to convert suffering into strength.

When your Balance Track has **3 or more boxes filled**, you gain **+1 to Foriti rolls**.

The more you're struggling, the harder you push.

### 3. Sacred Endurance

**Prerequisites:** Foriti +1

**Effect:** When performing **physically or spiritually demanding Rituals**, you gain **+1 to the roll**.

You've trained your body and spirit to handle intense exertion.

### 4. Fearless Confrontation

**Prerequisites:** Foriti +2

**Effect:** When facing **hostile or frightening spirits** directly, you gain **+1 to Foriti rolls**.

You've stared down too many terrors to be easily shaken.

### 5. Second Wind

**Prerequisites:** Foriti +1

**Effect:** Once per session, when you fail a Foriti roll (6-) and would gain Imbalance, you can **dig deeper**.

Re-roll the dice. If the second roll is **7+**, you succeed and ignore the Imbalance from the first roll.

If the second roll is also a failure, you gain **2 Imbalance (Fear)** instead of 1—you pushed too hard.

### 6. Guardian's Stand

**Prerequisites:** Foriti +2, Warrior-Poet or Mediator Archetype

**Effect:** When **protecting another person** from spiritual harm, you can **automatically succeed** at preventing them from gaining Imbalance—but you take it onto yourself instead.

You don't roll. You simply absorb the hit.

**Limit:** Once per session.

### 7. Iron Will *(Passive)*

**Prerequisites:** Foriti +2

**Effect:** You are **immune to psychic intimidation and fear-based manipulation**.

Spirits cannot frighten you into compliance. Social pressure doesn't shake you. You can't be coerced through fear.

**Note:** You can still feel fear (and gain Fear Imbalance from trials), but others can't *use* fear against you.

### 8. Titan's Strength

**Prerequisites:** Foriti +2, must have achieved at least **5 Breakthroughs**

**Effect:** Once per Mystery, you can attempt something that should be **physically or spiritually impossible**.

You declare: **"I break through."**

Make a Foriti roll:

- **10+:** You succeed. The impossible becomes possible.

- **7-9:** You succeed, but mark **3 Imbalance (Fear)** from the effort.

- **6-:** You fail, and mark **2 Imbalance (Fear)** from trying.

**Examples:** Carrying a massive spiritual burden alone, pushing through a sealed threshold, enduring a Ritual meant for multiple people.

### ATTUNEMENTS OF IWA (INTEGRITY)

These Attunements require **Iwa +1 or higher** unless otherwise noted.

### 1. Truth Incarnate *(Passive)*

**Prerequisites:** Iwa +1

**Effect:** You **cannot lie**.

Not "you choose not to"—you literally **cannot**. If you attempt to speak a falsehood, the words won't form.

**Benefit:** Because of this, people trust you implicitly. You gain **+1 to all social rolls** where honesty is relevant.

**Drawback:** Sometimes the truth is dangerous. Choose this Attunement carefully.

### 2. Oath-Keeper

**Prerequisites:** Iwa +1

**Effect:** When you make a **formal promise or oath**, it gains spiritual weight.

If you **keep** the promise, you gain **+1 to your next Ebo roll**.

If you **break** the promise, you immediately gain **2 Imbalance (Ego)** and cannot clear Ego until you make amends.

### 3. Selfless Service

**Prerequisites:** Iwa +1

**Effect:** When you perform Ebo that involves **anonymous service** (helping others without recognition or reward), you gain **+1 to the roll**.

Your humility is genuine, and the spirits honor it.

### 4. Moral Clarity *(Passive)*

**Prerequisites:** Iwa +2

**Effect:** You always know **the right action** in any given situation.

You may not always be able to take it (circumstances, fear, complexity), but you always *know* what integrity demands.

Once per session, you can ask the Guide: **"What is the right thing to do here?"** They will tell you the truth.

### 5. Witness of Truth

**Prerequisites:** Iwa +1, Truth-Speaker or Mediator Archetype

**Effect:** When you **speak truth publicly** (confessing fault, exposing deception, honoring someone's character), your words carry power.

Anyone listening must make an **Iwa roll** to resist being moved by your words. On a failure, they are compelled to acknowledge the truth—even if they don't like it.

### 6. Incorruptible *(Passive)*

**Prerequisites:** Iwa +2

**Effect:** You **cannot be bribed, tempted, or manipulated** into acting against your principles.

Spirits offering shortcuts? You see through it immediately.  
Someone offering power for compromise? Doesn't even tempt you.

You automatically succeed at any roll to **resist corruption or moral compromise**.

### 7. Sacred Accountability

**Prerequisites:** Iwa +2

**Effect:** Once per session, you can **hold someone to account** for breaking a promise, telling a lie, or acting without integrity.

When you confront them directly (in person, publicly, or through formal channels), they must roll **Iwa**. On a failure, they gain **1 Imbalance (Ego)** and feel compelled to admit fault or make amends.
### 8. Living Example *(Passive)*

**Prerequisites:** Iwa +2, must have achieved at least **5 Breakthroughs**

**Effect:** Your integrity is so evident that it inspires others.

Any Seeker who witnesses you **act with complete integrity** (keeping a hard promise, telling a costly truth, performing selfless service) gains **+1 to their next Iwa roll**.

Your character literally uplifts those around you.

### Creating Custom Attunements

As you play, you and your Guide might discover that your Seeker has developed a unique ability not covered in this list.

**That's great!** Work together to create a custom Attunement.

**Guidelines for Custom Attunements:**

**1. It should reflect something your Seeker has actually practiced.**  
If you've spent three Mysteries working with river-spirits, you might develop "River-Whisperer."

**2. It should be balanced with existing Attunements.**  
Don't make it stronger than what's listed here. Use these as templates.

**3. It should have clear prerequisites.**  
Tie it to a Virtue score, Archetype, or number of Breakthroughs.

**4. Write it down.**  
Add it to your character sheet so you and your Guide remember it.

**Example Custom Attunement:**

**"Data-Stream Surfer"** (Custom)  
*Prerequisites: Oye +1, Code-Smith Archetype, must have worked extensively with data-spirits*

*Effect: You can "ride" data-streams as a form of fast travel within the Temple's network. Once per session, you can instantaneously travel from one networked terminal to another by entering the data-stream and flowing with it. This takes moments but is disorienting—mark 1 Imbalance (Doubt) when you arrive.*

### Choosing Your Attunements

When you achieve a Breakthrough and choose to learn an Attunement, think about:

**What fits my Seeker's story?**  
Choose Attunements that reflect what your Seeker has been learning, practicing, or struggling with.

**What do I need mechanically?**  
If you keep running out of Balance, take Resilient Heart. If you struggle with Foriti, take Pain is Fuel.

**What excites me as a player?**  
Choose cool abilities that make you smile. This is a game. Have fun.

**What makes my Seeker unique?**  
Two Healers with different Attunements will feel completely different in play.
